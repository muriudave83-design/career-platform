# How to Answer "Why Should We Hire You?" in an Interview (Kenya 2026)

Few interview questions ask you to summarise your value as directly as:

> **"Why should we hire you?"**

For many candidates, it's also one of the most intimidating.

It can feel uncomfortable to talk about your own strengths, especially when you're sitting across from experienced recruiters or hiring managers.

As a result, many people give vague answers such as:

- "I'm hardworking."
- "I'm passionate."
- "I'll do my best."
- "I really need this job."

While these statements may be sincere, they don't explain **why you are the right person for this specific role**.

That's what interviewers are really trying to understand.

This question isn't about confidence alone.

It's about whether you can clearly connect your skills, experience and potential to the organisation's needs.

# Quick Answer

Interviewers ask **"Why should we hire you?"** because they want to understand the value you'll bring to their organisation.

A strong answer should:

- demonstrate that you understand the role
- highlight your most relevant strengths
- support your claims with evidence
- explain how you'll contribute to the organisation's goals
- show genuine enthusiasm for the opportunity

The most effective responses don't rely on generic statements.

Instead, they clearly connect **your abilities** with **the employer's needs**.

In the rest of this guide, you'll learn how to do that using the **JoinNexiva IMPACT™ Framework**.

# Why Recruiters Ask This Question

Although the wording varies, every version of this question is trying to answer one thing:

> **"If we hire you instead of another candidate, what value will we gain?"**

Recruiters aren't asking you to prove that you're perfect.

They're asking you to explain why you're the best fit for **this** position.

Behind your answer, they're assessing:

- your understanding of the role
- your confidence
- your communication skills
- your self-awareness
- your ability to connect your experience to business needs

> **JoinNexiva Recruiter Insight**
>
> The strongest candidates don't simply list their qualities. They explain how those qualities solve problems or create value for the employer.

# The JoinNexiva IMPACT™ Framework

One of the biggest mistakes candidates make is focusing only on themselves.

Strong answers focus on the employer as well.

Use the **JoinNexiva IMPACT™ Framework** to structure your response.

| Step | Meaning | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I** | **Identify** | Identify what the employer is looking for. |
| **M** | **Match** | Match your skills, education or experience to those needs. |
| **P** | **Prove** | Support your claims with a real example or achievement. |
| **A** | **Add Value** | Explain the contribution you expect to make. |
| **C** | **Connect** | Show why this organisation fits your long-term goals. |
| **T** | **Thank** | Finish confidently and positively. |

Unlike generic interview advice, the IMPACT™ Framework reminds you that the conversation is not just about **who you are**—it's about **how you can help the organisation succeed**.
# What Recruiters Are Really Looking For

Many candidates assume this question is asking:

> "Why are you a good person?"

It isn't.

The interviewer is really asking:

> **"Why are you the best fit for this role compared to everyone else we've interviewed?"**

This is an important distinction.

Recruiters already know that many candidates are hardworking, motivated and eager to learn.

Those qualities are expected.

What separates outstanding candidates is their ability to explain **how those qualities will create value for the organisation**.

Think of your answer as your professional value proposition.

You're answering three questions simultaneously:

1. What can you do?
2. How have you demonstrated it?
3. How will it benefit this organisation?

If your answer clearly addresses those three questions, you've already distinguished yourself from many other candidates.

# Step 1: Identify What the Employer Needs

The first step of the **JoinNexiva IMPACT™ Framework** is often overlooked.

Before talking about yourself, understand what the employer is actually looking for.

Every job advert contains clues.

Review the responsibilities and requirements carefully.

Look for repeated themes such as:

- communication
- teamwork
- customer service
- analytical thinking
- leadership
- problem-solving
- adaptability
- attention to detail

Those recurring themes tell you what the employer values most.

> **JoinNexiva Recruiter Insight**
>
> The best interview answers don't begin with the candidate.
>
> They begin with the employer's needs.

Use this simple decision process before your interview.

```text
Does the employer value this skill?

        │
        ▼

      No
        │
        ▼
Choose another strength.

        │

       Yes
        │
        ▼

Can you prove it?

        │

   No ─────────► Don't mention it.

        │

       Yes
        │
        ▼

Include it in your answer.
```

This simple exercise prevents one of the biggest interview mistakes:

Talking about strengths that aren't relevant to the role.

# Step 2: Match Your Experience

Once you've identified what the employer needs, show how your background matches those requirements.

You don't need years of experience.

You simply need relevant evidence.

For example:

## Internship Candidate

Employer wants:

- willingness to learn
- teamwork
- communication

You might discuss:

- university group projects
- industrial attachment
- student leadership
- volunteering

---

## Graduate Trainee

Employer wants:

- analytical thinking
- leadership potential
- initiative

Relevant experiences might include:

- research projects
- competitions
- internships
- leadership roles
- community projects

---

## Experienced Professional

Employer wants:

- project management
- leadership
- customer relationships
- operational improvement

Discuss measurable workplace achievements rather than listing responsibilities.

---

# Step 3: Prove Your Claims

This is where many interview answers fail.

Candidates often say:

> "I'm a good leader."

That's simply an opinion.

Instead, provide evidence.

For example:

> "During my final year at university, I coordinated a team of five students during our capstone project. We completed the project ahead of schedule and achieved one of the highest marks in our class."

Now your leadership is believable.

Whenever possible, support your strengths using:

- measurable achievements
- university projects
- internships
- volunteering
- workplace examples
- leadership experiences

Evidence always carries more weight than adjectives.

---

# Step 4: Add Value

This is the part many interview guides forget.

Don't stop after explaining what you've done.

Explain what you'll contribute if you're hired.

For example:

Instead of saying:

> "I'm organised."

Say:

> "Because I'm organised, I believe I can contribute by managing multiple priorities efficiently while supporting the team's objectives."

Notice the difference.

The second answer shifts the conversation from **you** to **the organisation**.

Recruiters appreciate candidates who think this way.

---

# Step 5: Connect Your Goals to the Organisation

Strong candidates explain why they want **this** opportunity.

Avoid generic statements such as:

> "I just need a job."

Instead, connect your goals with the employer.

For example:

> "I'm particularly interested in this graduate programme because of its structured learning opportunities and exposure to different business functions. I believe it provides the environment I need to continue developing while making meaningful contributions to your team."

This demonstrates preparation and genuine interest.

---

# Step 6: Finish Positively

The final part of the IMPACT™ Framework is simple.

End confidently.

For example:

> "I believe my academic background, practical experience and willingness to learn would allow me to contribute positively to your organisation, and I'd welcome the opportunity to continue developing while supporting your team's goals."

Notice that this ending is confident without sounding arrogant.

# Sample Answers Using the JoinNexiva IMPACT™ Framework

Reading about interview techniques is helpful.

Seeing those techniques applied is even better.

The following examples demonstrate how candidates at different career stages can answer **"Why should we hire you?"** using the **JoinNexiva IMPACT™ Framework**.

Remember that these are examples—not scripts.

Use them as inspiration to develop answers based on your own education, experience and career goals.

---

# Example 1: Internship Candidate

> **Interviewer:** "Why should we hire you?"
>
> **Candidate:**
>
> "Although I'm at the beginning of my career, I've consistently looked for opportunities to learn and contribute. During my industrial attachment, I quickly adapted to new responsibilities, supported my team with administrative tasks and received positive feedback for being dependable and eager to learn. I believe my willingness to learn, strong work ethic and positive attitude would allow me to contribute while developing the practical skills needed to grow within your organisation."

### Why This Answer Works

Applying the **IMPACT™ Framework**:

- **Identify:** Employer needs someone who can learn quickly.
- **Match:** Candidate highlights adaptability and reliability.
- **Prove:** References industrial attachment experience.
- **Add Value:** Explains how they'll contribute immediately.
- **Connect:** Shows commitment to learning and growth.
- **Thank:** Ends positively and professionally.

---

# Example 2: Graduate Trainee

> **Candidate:**
>
> "I believe I'm a strong fit for this graduate programme because it combines the analytical skills I've developed at university with opportunities to continue learning in a professional environment. During my final-year project, I led a team that completed our research ahead of schedule while maintaining high academic standards. That experience strengthened my leadership, planning and teamwork skills. I'm excited about the opportunity to bring those qualities to your organisation while continuing to grow through your graduate development programme."

### Why This Answer Works

The candidate:

- connects academic achievements to workplace expectations
- demonstrates leadership through evidence
- explains how those skills will benefit the employer
- shows enthusiasm for long-term development

---

# Example 3: Banking Position

> **Candidate:**
>
> "From researching your organisation, I understand that customer trust and service excellence are central to your success. During my previous customer-facing role, I consistently handled client enquiries professionally while maintaining accurate records and meeting performance targets. I believe my communication skills, attention to detail and commitment to excellent customer service would allow me to contribute positively to your team while continuing to develop within Kenya's banking sector."

### Why This Answer Works

Rather than simply saying they have customer service skills, the candidate:

- demonstrates knowledge of the employer
- supports their claims with experience
- links those abilities to business value

---

# Example 4: Government Position

> **Candidate:**
>
> "I'm particularly interested in this role because it provides an opportunity to contribute to public service while applying the organisational and administrative skills I've developed during my internship. I understand the importance of professionalism, accountability and accuracy when serving the public. Throughout my attachment, I managed records carefully, supported daily operations and consistently met deadlines. I believe those experiences have prepared me to contribute effectively to your department."

### Why This Answer Works

Government recruiters often value:

- professionalism
- accountability
- integrity
- reliability

This answer addresses each of those priorities.

---

# Example 5: NGO Position

> **Candidate:**
>
> "What attracts me to your organisation is the opportunity to contribute to meaningful community impact while continuing to develop professionally. During my volunteer work, I collaborated with people from diverse backgrounds, coordinated community activities and learned the importance of adaptability and communication. I believe those experiences, together with my commitment to continuous learning, would allow me to contribute positively to your programmes."

### Why This Answer Works

The candidate aligns their experience with values commonly associated with NGOs:

- collaboration
- community engagement
- adaptability
- service

---

# Example 6: ICT Position

> **Candidate:**
>
> "Technology changes rapidly, and one of my greatest strengths is learning new tools quickly. During my internship, I taught myself a new framework to support a project that had tight deadlines. That allowed me to contribute sooner than expected while improving my technical skills. I believe my ability to learn, solve problems and collaborate with others would allow me to add value to your development team from the beginning."

### Why This Answer Works

Technical employers appreciate candidates who demonstrate:

- curiosity
- continuous learning
- adaptability
- practical problem-solving

This answer provides evidence rather than making unsupported claims.

---

# Example 7: Experienced Professional

> **Candidate:**
>
> "Over the past five years, I've developed experience leading projects, improving operational processes and mentoring junior colleagues. In my current role, I helped redesign a reporting process that reduced turnaround times while improving accuracy. I'm confident those experiences have prepared me to contribute immediately to your organisation, and I'm excited by the opportunity to bring those skills to a team focused on continuous improvement."

### Why This Answer Works

Experienced candidates should focus less on potential and more on proven results.

Notice that the answer highlights:

- measurable contribution
- leadership
- business improvement
- future value

# Adapting Your Answer for Different Employers

Your answer should never be identical for every interview.

Before each interview, ask yourself:

- What qualities does this employer value most?
- Which of my experiences best demonstrate those qualities?
- Which achievement is most relevant to this role?
- How will my skills help this organisation achieve its goals?

Even small adjustments can make your answer feel thoughtful and tailored rather than generic.

# 15 Mistakes That Can Cost You the Job

Even highly qualified candidates sometimes struggle with this question—not because they lack the right skills, but because they present them poorly.

Avoid these common mistakes.

---

## 1. Giving a Generic Answer

Saying:

> "I'm hardworking."

or

> "I'm a fast learner."

without providing evidence doesn't help recruiters understand your value.

Always support your claims with examples.

---

## 2. Talking Only About Yourself

Many candidates spend their entire answer discussing their own achievements.

Remember:

The interviewer wants to know how those achievements will benefit **their organisation**.

---

## 3. Ignoring the Job Description

Your answer should reflect the employer's priorities.

If the role emphasises customer service, teamwork and communication, those themes should appear in your response.

---

## 4. Repeating Your CV

The interviewer has already read your CV.

Instead of listing your qualifications again, explain what they mean in practice.

---

## 5. Making Unsupported Claims

Statements like:

- "I'm an excellent leader."
- "I'm very innovative."
- "I'm highly organised."

need supporting evidence.

Show recruiters where you've demonstrated those qualities.

---

## 6. Sounding Arrogant

Confidence is important.

Arrogance is not.

Avoid statements like:

> "I'm obviously the best candidate."

Instead, focus on explaining the value you believe you can bring.

---

## 7. Being Too Humble

The opposite problem is equally common.

Don't downplay your achievements.

This question is your opportunity to communicate your strengths professionally.

---

## 8. Speaking for Too Long

Aim for approximately **60 to 90 seconds**.

A focused answer is usually more persuasive than a lengthy one.

---

## 9. Forgetting to Mention Results

Recruiters remember outcomes.

Whenever possible, explain what your actions achieved.

For example:

- improved efficiency
- completed projects early
- increased customer satisfaction
- solved a specific problem

---

## 10. Using the Same Answer Everywhere

Different employers value different qualities.

Tailor your answer for every interview.

---

## 11. Ignoring Company Research

Candidates who reference the organisation's values, mission or goals immediately demonstrate preparation.

Spend time researching the employer before your interview.

---

## 12. Failing to Explain Your Motivation

Employers don't just want capable candidates.

They want people who genuinely want to work for their organisation.

---

## 13. Memorising a Script

Interviewers can usually tell when an answer has been memorised word for word.

Learn the structure—not the script.

The **IMPACT™ Framework** helps you answer naturally.

---

## 14. Forgetting to Smile

Non-verbal communication matters.

Maintain eye contact, smile naturally and speak with enthusiasm.

---

## 15. Ending Weakly

Don't finish with:

> "That's all."

End confidently.

Reinforce your interest in the opportunity and your enthusiasm for contributing.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## How Long Should My Answer Be?

Aim for **one to one-and-a-half minutes**.

That gives you enough time to explain your value without losing the interviewer's attention.

---

## Can I Mention More Than One Strength?

Yes.

However, it's usually better to discuss two or three strengths supported by evidence than to list many without explanation.

---

## What If I Don't Have Work Experience?

That's perfectly normal for internship and graduate candidates.

You can use examples from:

- university projects
- industrial attachments
- volunteering
- student leadership
- competitions
- community initiatives

Recruiters understand that early-career candidates build experience in different ways.

---

## Should I Mention Soft Skills or Technical Skills?

Ideally, include both.

Technical skills demonstrate competence.

Soft skills explain how you'll work with colleagues and contribute to the organisation.

---

## Is It Okay to Mention That I'm Willing to Learn?

Yes—but don't stop there.

Support your willingness to learn with an example showing that you've successfully learned something new in the past.
