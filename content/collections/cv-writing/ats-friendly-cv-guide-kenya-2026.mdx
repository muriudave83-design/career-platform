# ATS-Friendly CV Guide (2026): How to Optimise Your CV for Applicant Tracking Systems

Many job seekers worry that their CV is rejected before a recruiter ever reads it.

This concern has led to widespread discussion about Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS), but it has also created confusion. Some articles claim ATS automatically rejects candidates based on missing keywords. Others encourage applicants to copy entire job descriptions into their CVs or hide keywords in white text.

Most of this advice is misleading.

An ATS is not designed to replace recruiters. Its primary purpose is to organise applications, make candidate information searchable and help recruitment teams manage large volumes of applicants more efficiently.

Understanding how these systems actually work allows you to optimise your CV without sacrificing readability or professionalism.

This guide explains what an Applicant Tracking System is, how employers in Kenya use it, the mistakes that reduce your chances of being shortlisted and practical steps you can take to create an ATS-friendly CV that also impresses human recruiters.

---

## Quick Answer

An ATS-friendly CV uses a clean layout, standard section headings, relevant keywords and consistent formatting so recruitment software can accurately read your information. However, your CV must also remain clear, truthful and persuasive because recruiters—not software—make the final hiring decisions.

---

## Quick ATS Checklist

Before submitting your CV, confirm that:

- You have tailored your CV to the job description.
- Standard section headings are used.
- Formatting is clean and consistent.
- Fonts are professional and readable.
- Skills are supported by evidence.
- Keywords appear naturally.
- Graphics do not replace important information.
- The file format matches the employer's instructions.
- Spelling and grammar have been checked.
- Your CV is easy for both software and recruiters to understand.

## What Is an Applicant Tracking System?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is recruitment software that helps organisations collect, organise and search job applications.

Instead of managing hundreds or thousands of CVs manually, recruiters use ATS platforms to:

- Receive applications.
- Store candidate information.
- Search for qualifications and skills.
- Track candidates through the recruitment process.
- Share applications with hiring managers.
- Schedule interviews.
- Record recruitment decisions.

The ATS acts as a recruitment management system rather than an automatic hiring decision-maker.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Recruiters still make hiring decisions. The ATS helps them organise applications more efficiently, but it does not replace professional judgment.

---

## Why Employers Use ATS

Recruitment has become increasingly digital.

A single vacancy can attract hundreds—or even thousands—of applications.

Without recruitment software, managing this volume would be slow and inefficient.

An ATS allows employers to:

- Organise applications in one place.
- Search for candidates with specific skills.
- Filter applications based on objective criteria.
- Reduce administrative work.
- Maintain recruitment records.
- Collaborate with hiring managers throughout the recruitment process.

For large organisations, the ATS improves efficiency rather than replacing recruiters.

## Do Employers in Kenya Use ATS?

Yes.

Many multinational companies, banks, telecommunications providers, large NGOs, manufacturing companies and major corporations operating in Kenya use Applicant Tracking Systems as part of their recruitment process.

However, not every employer uses the same software.

Some organisations rely on enterprise recruitment platforms, while smaller employers may review applications manually or use simpler recruitment tools.

This means your CV should always be written for **both** software **and** people.

## How Recruiters Actually Use ATS

One of the biggest misconceptions is that recruiters simply upload CVs and allow software to choose who gets hired.

That is rarely how recruitment works.

A typical recruitment process looks like this:

| Stage | What Happens |
|--------|--------------|
| Application | Candidates submit their CVs and supporting documents. |
| ATS Processing | Candidate information is organised and indexed. |
| Recruiter Review | Recruiters search, filter and review suitable applications. |
| Shortlisting | Candidates meeting the role's requirements are shortlisted. |
| Interviews | Hiring managers assess shortlisted candidates. |
| Selection | Final hiring decisions are made by people—not software. |

The ATS supports recruiters.

It does not replace them.

## ATS Myths vs Reality

Many myths about ATS continue to circulate online.

Understanding the difference helps you focus on improvements that genuinely matter.

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| ATS automatically rejects every CV. | Recruiters usually decide who progresses. |
| More keywords always improve rankings. | Keywords must appear naturally and accurately. |
| Hidden white-text keywords improve your chances. | This is considered deceptive and may damage credibility. |
| Fancy designs always fail ATS. | Simple designs are generally safer, but modern ATS platforms can handle many layouts. |
| ATS hires people. | Recruiters and hiring managers make hiring decisions. |

Understanding these realities allows you to optimise your CV without relying on ineffective tactics.

## Use the Right Keywords Naturally

One of the biggest misconceptions about Applicant Tracking Systems is that you need to "beat the algorithm" by filling your CV with as many keywords as possible.

That approach rarely works.

Recruiters—and modern ATS platforms—are looking for relevance, not repetition.

The most effective way to optimise your CV is to use the same professional language that appears in the job advertisement where it accurately reflects your own experience.

For example, if a vacancy requests:

- Microsoft Excel
- Financial Analysis
- Budget Preparation
- Data Analysis

Your CV should naturally include those skills if you genuinely possess them.

Avoid adding skills you cannot demonstrate during an interview.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Recruiters frequently ask candidates to explain the skills listed on their CV. Including keywords you cannot support usually becomes obvious during interviews.

---

## Tailor Every CV

No ATS strategy is more effective than tailoring your CV for each application.

Instead of sending one generic CV to every employer:

- Read the job description carefully.
- Identify the required skills.
- Update your professional summary.
- Highlight your most relevant experience.
- Reorder sections where appropriate.
- Remove information unrelated to the role.

For example, if you're applying for a software engineering internship, your programming projects should appear before unrelated volunteer activities.

If you're applying for a finance role, financial analysis projects and Excel skills should receive greater emphasis.

Tailoring improves both recruiter experience and ATS relevance.

---

## Use Standard Section Headings

Applicant Tracking Systems recognise common CV structures more reliably than creative alternatives.

Use headings such as:

- Professional Summary
- Education
- Professional Experience
- Projects
- Skills
- Certifications
- Leadership Experience
- Volunteer Experience
- Awards
- Referees

Avoid unusual headings such as:

- My Journey
- My Story
- Things I Have Done
- Career Highlights (when used instead of Experience)
- What Makes Me Different

Clear headings improve readability for both software and recruiters.

---

## Make Your Experience Easy to Understand

Recruiters should immediately understand:

- Your role.
- Your organisation.
- Your dates.
- Your responsibilities.
- Your achievements.

A simple structure works well.

**ICT Intern**

ABC Technologies

June 2025 – August 2025

- Supported first-line technical support.
- Assisted with workstation deployment.
- Documented recurring technical issues.
- Participated in network maintenance.

Avoid long paragraphs whenever possible.

Bullet points improve scanning and readability.

---

## Formatting That Works Well With ATS

Most ATS platforms perform best when documents use clean, consistent formatting.

Recommended practices include:

### Use

- Standard fonts.
- Black text.
- Left alignment.
- Consistent spacing.
- Simple bullet points.
- Logical heading hierarchy.

### Avoid

- Decorative borders.
- Text boxes containing important information.
- Images replacing text.
- Unnecessary icons.
- Complex page layouts.

Simple formatting also makes your CV easier for recruiters to review quickly.

---

## PDF or Word?

This question has become increasingly common.

The correct answer depends on the employer's instructions.

| Employer Instruction | Recommended Format |
|----------------------|--------------------|
| PDF requested | Submit PDF |
| Word requested | Submit Word (.docx) |
| No preference stated | PDF is generally the safest option |

Modern ATS platforms can usually process well-formatted PDF documents.

However, if an employer specifically requests Word format, always follow those instructions.

Following application instructions demonstrates professionalism.

---

## Should You Use Tables?

Simple tables used sparingly are generally acceptable.

However, avoid building your entire CV inside large or complex tables.

Instead, use tables only where they genuinely improve clarity.

For example:

| Skill Category | Skills |
|----------------|--------|
| Programming | Java, Python, JavaScript |
| Databases | PostgreSQL, MySQL |
| Productivity | Microsoft Excel, Word, PowerPoint |

Your primary content should remain easy to read in a normal text flow.

---

## Are Columns Safe?

Many modern ATS platforms can interpret two-column layouts.

However, compatibility varies between recruitment systems.

If you're applying to a large organisation or are unsure which ATS is being used, a clean single-column layout remains the safest choice.

Single-column layouts also perform well on mobile devices and simplify recruiter review.

---

## Should You Use Icons?

Icons are not inherently harmful.

However, they should never replace important text.

For example:

Good:

📧 brian@example.com

Poor:

📧 (without the actual email address)

Always ensure your contact information exists as readable text.

---

## Graphics and Infographics

Infographic CVs often look attractive but can reduce readability.

For most industries—including finance, engineering, healthcare, government and technology—a simple professional layout remains the better choice.

Creative professionals may include portfolios separately while keeping the CV itself straightforward.

> **Career Coach Tip**
>
> Your portfolio demonstrates creativity. Your CV demonstrates professionalism. Let each document serve its purpose.

## Choose a Professional Filename

The filename is one of the first things recruiters see.

Instead of:

- CV Final Final.pdf
- Resume New.pdf
- Updated CV Latest.pdf

Use:

- Brian_Mwangi_CV.pdf
- Faith_Achieng_CV.pdf
- Kevin_Omondi_CV.pdf

Professional naming conventions create a stronger first impression.

## Common ATS Mistakes

Many applicants unintentionally reduce the effectiveness of their CV by making avoidable mistakes.

These include:

- Sending the same CV to every employer.
- Ignoring keywords from the job description.
- Using inconsistent formatting.
- Adding unsupported technical skills.
- Using unprofessional filenames.
- Including unnecessary graphics.
- Uploading the wrong file.

## ATS Myths That Continue to Mislead Job Seekers

Applicant Tracking Systems have become surrounded by myths.

Unfortunately, many of these myths encourage applicants to make changes that either have no effect or actually reduce the quality of their CV.

Let's separate fact from fiction.

### Myth 1: ATS Automatically Rejects Every CV

This is probably the most common misconception.

An ATS does not "decide" who deserves a job.

Its primary purpose is to organise applications, make candidate information searchable and help recruiters manage recruitment efficiently.

Recruiters still decide:

- Who gets shortlisted.
- Who progresses to interviews.
- Who receives an offer.

Your goal is to make your CV easy for both the ATS and the recruiter to understand.

---

### Myth 2: More Keywords Always Mean Better Results

Adding every keyword from the job description will not automatically improve your chances.

Recruiters can immediately recognise keyword stuffing.

Instead:

- Use relevant terminology naturally.
- Match your genuine skills to the role.
- Demonstrate those skills with evidence.

An honest, well-tailored CV is far more persuasive than one filled with repetitive keywords.

---

### Myth 3: Hidden Keywords Improve ATS Scores

Some websites recommend hiding keywords using white text or tiny fonts.

Do not do this.

Modern recruitment systems and recruiters may interpret hidden text as an attempt to manipulate the recruitment process.

It adds no real value and may damage your credibility.

---

### Myth 4: Creative Designs Always Fail ATS

Modern Applicant Tracking Systems are more capable than many people realise.

Simple design elements are usually acceptable.

The real issue is readability.

If your design makes important information difficult to extract or understand, both recruiters and software may struggle.

When in doubt, choose clarity over decoration.

---

### Myth 5: ATS Replaces Recruiters

Technology supports recruitment.

It does not replace professional judgment.

Recruiters consider:

- Experience.
- Qualifications.
- Communication.
- Cultural fit.
- Career progression.
- Interview performance.

Your CV opens the door.

People make the hiring decision.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> The strongest applications are rarely those that try to "beat the ATS." They are the ones that clearly demonstrate why the candidate can succeed in the role.

## How to Test Whether Your CV Is ATS-Friendly

Before submitting your application, review your CV objectively.

Ask yourself:

- Are section headings clear?
- Can someone understand my experience quickly?
- Are my achievements easy to identify?
- Is the formatting consistent?
- Have I tailored this CV for the role?
- Have I included relevant skills naturally?
- Is every claim truthful?

If the answer is "yes" to all of these questions, your CV is likely well prepared for both ATS software and recruiter review.

## Final ATS Optimisation Checklist

Before submitting your application, complete this checklist.

### Content

- My CV has been tailored to this role.
- I have used relevant terminology naturally.
- My skills match the vacancy.
- Every claim is truthful.
- My achievements are supported with evidence.

### Formatting

- Standard section headings are used.
- Font and spacing are consistent.

## Which ATS Platforms Do Employers Use?

Different employers use different recruitment systems.

Large multinational organisations may use enterprise ATS platforms, while smaller employers often rely on simpler recruitment software or manual review.

The specific software matters far less than following good CV writing principles. A clear, well-structured CV with relevant experience and professional formatting performs well across different recruitment systems.

Rather than trying to optimise your CV for one specific ATS, focus on making it easy for any recruiter to read and understand.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Does Every Employer Use ATS?

No.

Many large organisations use Applicant Tracking Systems, while smaller employers may review applications manually or use simpler recruitment platforms.

Your CV should therefore be easy for both software and recruiters to read.

---

## Should I Copy the Job Description Into My CV?

No.

Your CV should reflect your own qualifications and experience.

Use relevant terminology naturally where it accurately describes your skills and achievements.

---

## Can ATS Read PDF Files?

Most modern Applicant Tracking Systems can process well-formatted PDF documents.

However, always follow the employer's instructions if they request another file format.

---

## Are Graphics Bad for ATS?

Not necessarily.

The problem arises when important information is presented only as images or decorative elements.

Simple, text-based layouts remain the safest option.

---

## Should Every Application Have a Different CV?

Ideally, yes.

Small adjustments to your professional summary, skills and highlighted achievements make your CV more relevant to each vacancy.

Tailoring consistently produces better results than sending the same generic CV everywhere.

---

## Is ATS Optimisation More Important Than Content?

No.

Excellent formatting cannot compensate for weak content.

Your qualifications, achievements, projects and experience remain the most important parts of your CV.

ATS optimisation simply ensures that information is presented clearly.

# Key Takeaways

Applicant Tracking Systems are recruitment tools—not hiring managers.

The strongest ATS-friendly CVs focus on clarity, relevance and professionalism rather than gimmicks or keyword stuffing.

Remember these principles:

- Tailor every CV.
- Use standard headings.
- Keep formatting simple.
- Demonstrate skills with evidence.
- Use keywords naturally.
- Follow employer instructions.
- Optimise for recruiters first and software second.

When your CV is both ATS-friendly and recruiter-friendly, you maximise your chances of progressing to the interview stage.
