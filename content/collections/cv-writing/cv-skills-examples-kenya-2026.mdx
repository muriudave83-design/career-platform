# CV Skills Examples (2026): 200+ Skills to Include on Your CV by Industry

Choosing the right skills for your CV is about far more than filling space on the page.

Recruiters use your skills section to quickly evaluate whether you have the knowledge, technical ability and personal qualities needed to perform the role. Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) also scan for relevant skills to determine whether your application matches the requirements of a vacancy.

The challenge is that many job seekers either include too few skills or list every skill they can think of without considering whether it is relevant. A long, unfocused list can be just as ineffective as leaving the section almost empty.

The strongest CVs present carefully selected skills that align with the job description and are supported by evidence elsewhere in the application. Instead of simply claiming to be organised, analytical or a good communicator, successful candidates demonstrate those qualities through their education, work experience, projects and achievements.

In this guide, you'll learn how recruiters evaluate skills, how Applicant Tracking Systems identify relevant keywords and how to choose the skills that genuinely strengthen your CV. You'll also find more than 200 examples organised by career stage and industry to help you build a skills section that reflects your experience and the role you're applying for.

Whether you're writing your first CV as a student, applying for graduate opportunities or updating your CV after years of professional experience, this guide will help you present your abilities with confidence.

---

## Quick Answer

The best skills to include on your CV are those that are:

- Relevant to the position.
- Supported by evidence from your experience.
- Recognised within your industry.
- Current and up to date.
- Demonstrated through measurable achievements whenever possible.

Aim for a balanced combination of:

- Technical or hard skills.
- Transferable skills.
- Interpersonal (soft) skills.
- Industry-specific knowledge.

Every skill should help answer one question:

> **"Can this candidate perform the responsibilities of this role?"**

---

## Why the Skills Section Matters

Recruiters rarely read a CV from beginning to end on the first review.

Instead, they scan for indicators that the candidate meets the essential requirements of the position.

One of the fastest ways to do this is by reviewing the skills section.

A well-written skills section allows recruiters to identify:

- Technical competence.
- Professional strengths.
- Industry knowledge.
- Tools and software proficiency.
- Transferable abilities.
- Alignment with the vacancy.

For candidates with limited work experience, the skills section often carries even greater weight because it demonstrates readiness to contribute despite a shorter employment history.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Recruiters are not looking for the candidate with the longest list of skills. They are looking for the candidate with the most relevant skills, supported by credible evidence elsewhere in the CV.

## Understanding Hard Skills and Soft Skills

Many applicants have heard the terms *hard skills* and *soft skills*, but understanding the difference helps you create a stronger, more balanced CV.

### What Are Hard Skills?

Hard skills are measurable abilities that can usually be learned through education, training or practical experience.

Examples include:

- Programming languages.
- Financial reporting.
- Data analysis.
- Graphic design.
- Electrical installation.
- Project management software.
- AutoCAD.
- Microsoft Excel.
- SQL.
- Digital marketing.

These skills are often listed directly in job descriptions and are among the easiest for recruiters and ATS software to identify.

### What Are Soft Skills?

Soft skills describe how you work with people, solve problems and manage your responsibilities.

Examples include:

- Communication.
- Teamwork.
- Leadership.
- Adaptability.
- Critical thinking.
- Time management.
- Customer service.
- Conflict resolution.
- Emotional intelligence.
- Creativity.

Unlike hard skills, soft skills are most convincing when demonstrated through achievements rather than listed without context.

## How Recruiters Evaluate Skills

Listing a skill on your CV does not automatically convince a recruiter that you possess it.

Instead, recruiters look for consistency across your application.

For example, if you claim to have strong project management skills, they expect to see supporting evidence such as:

- Leading university projects.
- Coordinating workplace initiatives.
- Managing deadlines.
- Delivering measurable outcomes.

Similarly, if you list Microsoft Excel as an advanced skill, recruiters may expect examples involving:

- Financial analysis.
- Reporting dashboards.
- Data cleaning.
- Pivot tables.
- Forecasting.
- Budget preparation.

The strongest CVs don't just state skills—they prove them.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Every important skill listed in your skills section should appear naturally elsewhere in your CV through your education, work experience, projects or achievements.

---

## How Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) Read Skills

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems to organise and search applications before recruiters review them.

These systems compare the wording in your CV with the requirements of the job description.

For example, if a vacancy requests:

- SQL
- Power BI
- Financial reporting
- Budget forecasting

but your CV only mentions:

- Database knowledge
- Reporting software
- Finance

the system may not recognise the connection as effectively.

Whenever appropriate, use the same terminology found in the job advertisement, provided it accurately reflects your experience.

This improves both ATS compatibility and clarity for human recruiters.

Remember, however, that keyword optimisation should never come at the expense of honesty. Only include skills that you can confidently demonstrate if invited for an interview.

---

## A Simple Formula for Choosing the Right Skills

Before adding a skill to your CV, ask yourself four questions:

1. Is this skill relevant to the role?
2. Can I demonstrate this skill with evidence?
3. Is this skill current and recognised within my industry?
4. Would I confidently discuss or demonstrate this skill during an interview?

If the answer to any of these questions is "no," consider whether the skill truly strengthens your application.

Using this simple filter helps you create a focused skills section that supports your overall CV rather than distracting from it.

A carefully selected list of relevant skills is always more persuasive than an exhaustive list of unrelated abilities.

# How to Prove Your Skills Instead of Simply Listing Them

Many applicants make the mistake of treating the skills section as a checklist. Recruiters, however, look for evidence that supports each claim.

Consider the difference below.

### Weak Example

```text
Skills

• Leadership
• Communication
• Microsoft Excel
• Teamwork
• Problem-solving
```

This tells the recruiter very little about how those skills have been applied.

### Strong Example

```text
Skills

• Advanced Microsoft Excel (pivot tables, dashboards and financial reporting)

• Leadership (led a five-member university project team)

• Communication (delivered presentations to audiences of more than 100 students)

• Problem-solving (developed an inventory tracking solution during internship)

• Teamwork (collaborated with cross-functional teams during industrial attachment)
```

The second example is far more persuasive because it provides context and credibility.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Every important skill should appear naturally elsewhere in your CV through your work experience, projects, education or achievements. Evidence builds trust.

# Common Skills Section Mistakes

Avoid these common errors.

### Listing Every Skill You Know

A long list of unrelated skills makes it difficult for recruiters to identify your strengths.

Prioritise relevance over quantity.

---

### Including Outdated Skills

Skills that were once valuable may no longer strengthen a modern CV if they are considered basic expectations.

Examples include:

- Basic internet browsing
- Basic email use
- Basic Microsoft Word

Replace these with more advanced or role-specific capabilities where appropriate.

---

### Exaggerating Your Ability

Never claim expertise in software, tools or technical areas you cannot confidently discuss or demonstrate.

Recruiters may assess these skills during interviews or practical tests.

---

### Ignoring the Job Description

One of the simplest ways to strengthen your CV is to compare your skills with those requested in the vacancy.

Tailor your skills section for each application while remaining truthful.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## How Many Skills Should I Include on My CV?

There is no fixed number, but most candidates benefit from including **10 to 20 carefully selected skills** that are relevant to the role.

---

## Should I Include Soft Skills?

Yes.

Soft skills such as communication, teamwork and problem-solving remain highly valued. However, they should be supported with examples elsewhere in your CV.

---

## Should I Rate My Skills?

Skill ratings (for example, "Excel – 5/5") are generally not recommended because they are subjective.

Instead, describe your level through practical experience or achievements.

---

## Can I Use the Same Skills Section for Every Job?

No.

Review the job description before each application and prioritise the skills that best match the employer's requirements.

---

## Should Students Include Technical Skills?

Yes.

Include technical skills gained through coursework, projects, certifications or personal learning, provided you can demonstrate your competence.
