# The Complete Guide to CV Writing in Kenya (2026)

Your Curriculum Vitae (CV) is often the first opportunity you have to convince an employer that you're worth interviewing.

Before a recruiter speaks to you, reads your cover letter or assesses your technical skills, they almost always review your CV. In many organisations, this first review lasts less than a minute. During that short time, your CV must clearly communicate who you are, what you've achieved and why you're a strong fit for the opportunity.

For Kenyan students, graduates and early-career professionals, writing a CV can feel challenging—especially when you have limited work experience. Many applicants assume they have little to include, while others try to compensate by creating overly long documents filled with unnecessary information.

Neither approach works.

An effective CV is not about listing everything you've ever done. It is about presenting the most relevant information in a way that makes it easy for recruiters to understand your potential.

Whether you're applying for an internship, industrial attachment, graduate trainee programme or your first full-time job, the principles in this guide will help you create a CV that meets modern recruitment expectations.

Rather than searching through multiple websites for conflicting advice, use this guide as your complete reference for CV writing in Kenya.

---

## Quick Answer

A strong Kenyan CV is clear, concise, relevant and tailored to the position you're applying for. Employers expect applicants to highlight their education, practical experience, technical skills, achievements and potential using a professional format that is easy to read and compatible with modern recruitment systems.

## Quick CV Checklist

Before sending your CV, make sure you can confidently answer **"Yes"** to each of these questions.

- Is my CV tailored to this specific opportunity?
- Is it no longer than two pages (or one page for most internship applications)?
- Have I used a professional email address?
- Have I removed all spelling and grammar mistakes?
- Does my professional summary match the role I'm applying for?
- Have I included measurable achievements where possible?
- Are my skills relevant to the position?
- Is the formatting clean and consistent?
- Have I saved the file as a PDF unless instructed otherwise?
- Have I named the file professionally?

If any answer is **"No,"** improve your CV before submitting your application.

## Why Your CV Matters

Recruiters rarely have enough time to study every application in detail.

Instead, they perform an initial screening to decide which candidates deserve closer consideration.

Your CV answers several important questions immediately:

- Does this candidate meet the minimum qualifications?
- Do they have relevant experience or transferable skills?
- Have they demonstrated initiative?
- Can they communicate professionally?
- Is their background relevant to the vacancy?
- Would they likely succeed in this role?

A well-written CV helps recruiters answer these questions quickly.

A poorly organised CV forces them to search for information—and that often works against the applicant.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Recruiters are not looking for the "perfect" candidate during the first screening. They're looking for clear evidence that you meet the role's requirements and have the potential to perform well. A simple, organised CV usually creates a stronger impression than one filled with unnecessary graphics, colours or lengthy paragraphs.

## CV vs Résumé: Is There a Difference?

The terms **CV** and **résumé** are often used interchangeably, but they are not always identical.

In Kenya, employers generally request a **CV**, and the document you submit should provide a structured overview of your education, experience, skills and achievements.

| CV | Résumé |
|----|---------|
| Commonly requested by Kenyan employers | More common in some international markets |
| Provides a broader overview of your qualifications | Usually shorter and highly targeted |
| Suitable for internships, graduate programmes and most jobs | Often customised for a specific role |
| Typically one to two pages for early-career professionals | Usually limited to one page |

For most Kenyan internship and graduate opportunities, following the guidance in this article will meet employer expectations regardless of whether the advertisement refers to a CV or a résumé.

## Before You Start Writing

Many applicants open Microsoft Word and begin typing immediately.

That's usually the wrong approach.

The strongest CVs are planned before they're written.

Start by gathering the information you'll need:

- Academic qualifications.
- Internship experience.
- Industrial attachments.
- Volunteer work.
- Leadership positions.
- Academic projects.
- Technical projects.
- Certifications.
- Awards.
- Professional memberships.
- Referees (only if requested).

Next, carefully review the job advertisement.

Highlight the qualifications, skills and responsibilities the employer emphasises.

Your CV should demonstrate that you match those requirements wherever your experience genuinely supports them.

> **Decision Guide**
>
> Never write your CV in isolation from the job you're applying for. Read the vacancy first, identify the employer's priorities, then organise your CV to highlight your most relevant qualifications and achievements.

## The Anatomy of a Great Kenyan CV

Although every profession has its own expectations, most successful CVs for internships, graduate trainee programmes and entry-level jobs follow a similar structure.

A modern Kenyan CV should generally include:

1. Contact Information
2. Professional Summary
3. Education
4. Work Experience (if applicable)
5. Internships and Industrial Attachments
6. Projects
7. Technical and Professional Skills
8. Certifications
9. Leadership and Volunteer Experience
10. Awards and Achievements
11. Referees (only where requested)

Think of your CV as a story.

Each section should build confidence that you are capable of succeeding in the role you're applying for.

The order matters because recruiters naturally scan from top to bottom.

---

## Contact Information

Your contact details should appear at the very top of your CV.

Include:

- Full name
- Professional phone number
- Professional email address
- LinkedIn profile (if complete)
- Portfolio or GitHub (where relevant)
- County or town of residence (optional)

Example:

| Information | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| Name | Brian Mwangi |
| Phone | +254 712 345 678 |
| Email | brian.mwangi@email.com |
| LinkedIn | linkedin.com/in/brianmwangi |
| Portfolio | brianmwangi.dev |

Avoid including unnecessary personal information such as:

- National ID number
- Passport number
- Religion
- Tribe
- Marital status
- Height
- Weight
- Date of birth (unless specifically requested)

Modern employers focus on your qualifications rather than personal characteristics.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> A surprising number of applications are rejected simply because recruiters cannot easily contact the candidate. Always double-check your phone number and email address before sending your CV.

## Writing a Strong Professional Summary

The professional summary is one of the first sections recruiters read.

Its purpose is to explain who you are, what you can offer and the type of opportunity you're seeking.

For most students and graduates, three to five sentences are enough.

A strong summary should answer:

- Who are you?
- What are you studying or qualified in?
- What relevant experience do you have?
- What value can you bring?
- What type of opportunity are you pursuing?

### Example

> Final-year Computer Science student with practical experience developing web applications using JavaScript, React and PostgreSQL through academic and personal projects. Passionate about software engineering and continuous learning. Seeking an internship or graduate opportunity where I can contribute to real-world software development while expanding my technical skills.

Notice that the summary focuses on value rather than simply stating career ambitions.

### Common Mistakes

Avoid summaries such as:

> "I am a hardworking person looking for a challenging organisation where I can utilise my skills."

Recruiters read versions of this statement every day.

It says almost nothing about the candidate.

Instead, describe specific qualifications, skills and interests that relate directly to the role.

## Presenting Your Education

For students and recent graduates, education is usually one of the strongest sections of the CV.

Include:

- Institution
- Qualification
- Area of study
- Expected graduation date (or graduation year)
- Academic achievements where relevant

Example:

| Institution | Qualification |
|-------------|---------------|
| University of Nairobi | Bachelor of Science in Computer Science |
| Period | 2022–2026 |
| Status | Expected Graduation: December 2026 |

If you have limited work experience, you may also include:

- Relevant coursework
- Major academic projects
- Research work
- Final-year project

These demonstrate practical knowledge even before entering full-time employment.

> **Career Coach Tip**
>
> If a module directly relates to the role you're applying for—for example, Database Systems for a software internship or Financial Reporting for an accounting internship—you can reference it within your projects or experience rather than listing every course you've completed.

## Work Experience

Applicants often believe they should leave this section blank if they have never held a permanent job.

That's unnecessary.

Relevant experience includes:

- Part-time work
- Freelance projects
- Campus employment
- Volunteer work
- Student leadership roles
- Family business responsibilities (where relevant)
- Contract work

The key is demonstrating transferable skills.

For every role include:

- Organisation
- Position
- Employment period
- Three to five achievement-focused bullet points

Instead of writing:

- Assisted customers.

Write:

- Assisted more than 50 customers daily while maintaining high service standards and resolving enquiries efficiently.

Instead of:

- Managed inventory.

Write:

- Maintained inventory records and supported weekly stock reconciliation, improving reporting accuracy.

Recruiters prefer measurable achievements over lists of responsibilities.

## Internships and Industrial Attachments

Internships deserve their own section when they directly relate to the position you're applying for.

For each internship include:

- Organisation
- Department
- Duration
- Key responsibilities
- Achievements
- Systems or tools used
- Skills developed

For example:

**ICT Intern — ABC Bank**

June 2025 – August 2025

- Supported first-line technical support for over 150 employees.
- Assisted with hardware deployment and workstation setup.
- Documented recurring technical issues to improve internal knowledge resources.
- Collaborated with senior technicians during network maintenance.

Focus on what you accomplished rather than simply describing your daily duties.

If you haven't completed an internship yet, don't worry.

Your projects, coursework and leadership experience can still demonstrate your readiness for professional work.

## Showcasing Projects

Projects are one of the most overlooked sections of a graduate CV.

Yet they often provide the strongest evidence of your practical abilities.

Projects may include:

- University coursework
- Personal software applications
- Engineering designs
- Business case studies
- Marketing campaigns
- Data analysis projects
- Research projects
- Community initiatives

For every project answer four questions:

| Question | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| What problem did you solve? | Demonstrates understanding |
| What did you build or deliver? | Shows practical ability |
| Which tools or technologies did you use? | Highlights technical competence |
| What was the outcome? | Demonstrates impact |

Example:

**Student Management System**

Designed and developed a web-based student management platform using Next.js, PostgreSQL and Prisma ORM. Implemented secure authentication, role-based access control and reporting dashboards as part of a final-year software engineering project.

Projects allow recruiters to evaluate your skills even when you have limited professional experience.

For technical roles especially, a strong project section can become one of the most persuasive parts of your CV.

## Highlighting Your Skills

The skills section gives recruiters a quick overview of the abilities you bring to the role.

Instead of creating one long list, group similar skills together.

A clear structure is easier to scan and immediately communicates your strengths.

For example:

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Technical Skills | Microsoft Excel, SQL, Python, Java, React, AutoCAD |
| Digital Tools | Microsoft Office, Google Workspace, Canva, Power BI |
| Professional Skills | Communication, Teamwork, Problem Solving, Time Management |
| Languages | English, Kiswahili |

Only include skills you can confidently demonstrate during an interview or practical assessment.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Employers increasingly verify technical skills during interviews or practical tests. Listing a skill you cannot demonstrate damages credibility far more than omitting it altogether.

## Tailor Your Skills to the Job Description

One of the simplest ways to improve your CV is to compare your skills against the job advertisement.

For example:

| Employer Requests | Relevant Skills to Highlight |
|-------------------|-----------------------------|
| Microsoft Excel | Excel, Pivot Tables, Data Analysis |
| Customer Service | Communication, Conflict Resolution |
| Research | Data Collection, Report Writing |
| Programming | JavaScript, Python, SQL |
| Digital Marketing | SEO, Google Analytics, Social Media |

Your CV should make it easy for recruiters to see the connection between your experience and the role.

## Leadership Experience

Leadership is valuable even when it comes from university or community activities.

Examples include:

- Class representative
- Club chairperson
- Society committee member
- Student mentor
- Event organiser
- Team captain

Employers often associate leadership roles with initiative, responsibility and communication skills.

Instead of writing:

- Chairperson of Computer Society.

Write:

- Led a committee of twelve members to organise technical workshops attended by more than 300 students throughout the academic year.

Achievements demonstrate impact.

Responsibilities simply describe what was expected.

Achievements help distinguish your CV from applicants with similar qualifications.

## Should You Include Referees?

Many Kenyan employers still request referees.

However, unless the vacancy specifically asks for them, you do not need to include full referee details on every CV.

Instead, you may simply state:

> **Referees available upon request.**

If referee details are requested, include:

- Full name
- Job title
- Organisation
- Phone number
- Professional email address

Always ask for permission before listing someone as your referee.

## Formatting Your CV

Presentation matters.

A well-formatted CV is easier to read and reflects professionalism.

Follow these guidelines:

- Use a clean, professional font.
- Keep font size between 10 and 12 points.
- Maintain consistent spacing.
- Use clear section headings.
- Leave sufficient white space.
- Save as PDF unless instructed otherwise.
- Keep formatting simple and consistent.

Avoid:

- Bright colours
- Decorative fonts
- Excessive graphics
- Tables used purely for decoration
- Multiple font styles
- Unnecessary icons

Your content—not visual effects—should capture the recruiter's attention.

## Writing an ATS-Friendly CV

Many medium and large organisations now use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to organise applications before recruiters review them.

An ATS scans your CV for relevant information such as:

- Job titles
- Skills
- Qualifications
- Keywords
- Certifications

A modern ATS-friendly CV should:

- Use standard section headings.
- Avoid complex layouts.
- Use editable text.
- Match relevant keywords naturally.
- Avoid unnecessary graphics.
- Use clear formatting.

Contrary to popular belief, ATS optimisation is not about stuffing keywords.

It is about making your CV easy for both software and recruiters to understand.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Recruiters ultimately make hiring decisions—not software. Optimise your CV for readability first, then ensure it contains the terminology employers use in their vacancy announcements.

## Common CV Mistakes That Cost Candidates Interviews

Recruiters often reject applications because of avoidable mistakes rather than a lack of qualifications.

Review your CV carefully before every application.

### Using One CV for Every Job

One of the biggest mistakes applicants make is sending exactly the same CV to every employer.

Different employers prioritise different skills, experiences and qualifications.

Tailor your CV by:

- Updating your professional summary.
- Highlighting the most relevant skills.
- Reordering experience where appropriate.
- Using language that reflects the vacancy.

A customised CV demonstrates genuine interest and increases your chances of being shortlisted.

---

### Including Irrelevant Information

Your CV should answer one question:

> **"Why should this employer interview me?"**

Information that doesn't help answer that question should generally be removed.

Examples include:

- Primary school education.
- Childhood achievements.
- Personal hobbies unrelated to the role.
- Excessive personal details.
- Unrelated work experience from many years ago.

Every section should strengthen your application.

---

### Spelling and Grammar Errors

Small mistakes create a poor first impression.

Before submitting your CV:

- Use a spell checker.
- Read the document aloud.
- Ask someone you trust to review it.
- Review the PDF version before sending.

Professional communication begins with a professional CV.

---

### Poor Formatting

A cluttered CV makes recruiters work harder.

Avoid:

- Large blocks of text.
- Inconsistent fonts.
- Uneven spacing.
- Poor alignment.
- Excessive bold text.
- Decorative graphics.

Good formatting improves readability without distracting from your content.

---

### Exaggerating Experience

Never claim skills or achievements you cannot support.

Interviewers frequently ask follow-up questions about:

- Projects.
- Technical skills.
- Leadership experience.
- Employment history.

Honesty builds credibility.

Exaggeration destroys it.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## How Long Should a CV Be?

For most students, graduates and internship applicants, one page is usually sufficient.

Early-career professionals with several years of relevant experience may use two pages.

Only include information that strengthens your application.

---

## Should I Include My KCSE Results?

Usually not.

Once you have completed a diploma or university qualification, employers are generally more interested in your highest level of education and relevant experience.

Include secondary school results only if the employer specifically requests them.

---

## Should I Include a Photograph?

Unless the employer explicitly requests one, avoid including photographs.

Recruiters should evaluate your qualifications rather than your appearance.

---

## Can I Use AI to Write My CV?

AI can help you improve grammar, organise information and identify stronger wording.

However, every statement on your CV must accurately represent your own experience, achievements and abilities.

Never submit AI-generated information that is inaccurate or misleading.

---

## Should I Include Hobbies?

Only if they strengthen your application or demonstrate relevant qualities.

Examples include:

- Volunteer work.
- Competitive sports.
- Professional associations.
- Community leadership.

Avoid listing hobbies that do not contribute to your professional profile.

---

## Is a Cover Letter Still Important?

Yes.

Many employers continue to request cover letters, particularly for internships, graduate trainee programmes and entry-level positions.

A strong CV and a tailored cover letter complement one another.

Learn more in our guide to writing internship cover letters.

# Key Takeaways

A strong CV does not guarantee a job.

It does increase your chances of securing interviews by presenting your qualifications clearly, professionally and persuasively.

Remember these principles:

- Tailor every CV.
- Keep your layout simple.
- Focus on achievements rather than responsibilities.
- Demonstrate relevant skills.
- Include practical experience wherever possible.
- Proofread carefully.
- Keep learning and updating your CV as your career develops.

Your CV should evolve alongside your professional journey.

Review it regularly rather than updating it only when you need a job.
