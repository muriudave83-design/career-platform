# How to Find Internships in Kenya (2026)

Finding an internship in Kenya is rarely about luck.

Students who consistently secure internship opportunities usually have a system. They know where employers advertise, they prepare their application documents early, and they search every week instead of only when attachment becomes a graduation requirement.

Many students, on the other hand, begin searching only after exams end. By then, some organisations have already completed recruitment, while others are reviewing applications that were submitted months earlier.

The encouraging news is that internship opportunities exist throughout the year across many industries. Banks recruit interns. Technology companies recruit interns. NGOs recruit interns. Manufacturers recruit interns. County governments, research institutions, media houses, hospitals and consulting firms all offer opportunities at different times.

The challenge is knowing where to look and how to organise your search.

This guide explains practical strategies that Kenyan university students, TVET students and fresh graduates can use to find internships in 2026.

Whether you're studying engineering at JKUAT, accounting at Kenyatta University, nursing at KMTC, journalism at Daystar University, or software development at a TVET institution, the principles in this guide remain the same.

Start here if you're new to internships

Before searching for opportunities, read The Complete Guide to Internships in Kenya (Article #001) to understand the different types of internships, industrial attachments and graduate internship programmes available in Kenya.

Before You Start Searching

Many students believe the internship search begins when they open a job website.

In reality, it begins much earlier.

Recruiters often spend less than a minute reviewing an application during the first screening. If your documents are incomplete or unprofessional, you may be rejected before your qualifications are even considered.

Preparing properly before you start searching saves time and increases your chances of success.

Decide Which Opportunities Fit Your Career Goals

Avoid applying for every internship you see.

Instead, ask yourself:

Which industry interests me?
Which skills do I want to develop?
What type of work would I enjoy?
Which organisations operate in that field?

For example, a computer science student might prioritise software companies, fintech firms, telecommunications providers and digital agencies instead of applying randomly across unrelated sectors.

Having a clear direction helps you write stronger applications because employers can see genuine interest in their industry.

Prepare Your CV Before Opportunities Appear

One of the biggest mistakes students make is waiting until an application deadline to start writing their CV.

Instead:

Create a professional internship CV.
Update it after every project, attachment or volunteer experience.
Save both Word and PDF versions.
Keep the file name professional, such as Jane_Wanjiku_CV.pdf.

Pro Tip

Employers rarely extend internship deadlines because applicants are still preparing documents. A ready-to-send CV allows you to apply as soon as opportunities are published.

(Future Guide: Internship CV Guide)

Use a Professional Email Address

Your email address creates a first impression.

A format such as:

firstname.lastname@email.com

looks far more professional than nicknames or humorous usernames created years ago.

Also remember to:

Check your inbox regularly.
Enable notifications on your phone.
Review your spam folder occasionally.
Respond promptly to interview invitations.
Complete Your LinkedIn Profile

Many recruiters search LinkedIn before inviting candidates for interviews.

Even if you have limited experience, include:

Education
Technical skills
Student leadership
Volunteer work
Academic projects
Certifications
Awards

An incomplete profile can make you appear inactive, while a complete profile gives recruiters more information about your strengths.

Gather Important Documents Early

Different employers ask for different documents.

Create one folder containing copies of:

National ID (if applicable)
Academic transcripts
Result slips
Certificates
Recommendation letters (if available)
Portfolio (where relevant)

Having everything organised allows you to submit applications quickly without rushing to find documents at the last minute.

Why Students Struggle to Find Internships

Many students believe there simply are not enough internship opportunities.

While competition is real, recruiters often notice the same application mistakes year after year.

Understanding these challenges helps you avoid them.

Starting Too Late

Many employers recruit interns several months before the internship begins.

For example:

Internship Start	Recommended Time to Begin Searching
January	September–November
May	January–March
September	May–July

Beginning your search early gives you access to more opportunities before positions are filled.

Searching in Only One Place

Some students check a single website once every few weeks and assume they have seen every available opportunity.

In reality, employers advertise internships across multiple channels.

Some opportunities appear only on company websites.

Others are shared exclusively through universities, LinkedIn, professional associations or employee referrals.

Successful applicants search consistently across several platforms.

Sending Identical Applications

Recruiters can usually tell when the same CV and cover letter have been sent to dozens of employers.

Tailor your application by highlighting:

Relevant coursework
Technical skills
Projects
Volunteer experience
Achievements that relate to the advertised role

A targeted application demonstrates genuine interest.

Waiting for Opportunities Instead of Creating Them

Not every internship is advertised publicly.

Many smaller organisations are willing to host interns if approached professionally.

Students who combine advertised opportunities with networking and carefully written cold emails often discover opportunities that never appear on job boards.

Recruiter Insight

Hiring managers frequently receive applications after deadlines or with missing documents. Organisation, attention to detail and following instructions are often viewed as indicators of how a student will perform in the workplace.

Company Career Pages

Many of Kenya's largest employers publish internship opportunities on their own websites before they appear anywhere else.

Some organisations never post internships on external job boards at all.

If you only search on general job websites, you could miss excellent opportunities.

Why Employers Recruit Through Their Own Websites

Recruiting directly helps organisations:

Reach candidates interested in their company.
Reduce duplicate applications.
Keep all applications in one recruitment system.
Advertise opportunities before sharing them more widely.

For this reason, checking official career pages should become part of your weekly routine.

Build a Target Company List

Start by listing organisations where you would genuinely like to gain experience.

For example, you might group companies by industry.

Industry	Examples to Research
Banking	Commercial banks, microfinance institutions, investment firms
Technology	Software companies, fintech firms, telecommunications providers
Manufacturing	Consumer goods, industrial manufacturing, food processing
Healthcare	Hospitals, pharmaceutical companies, healthcare NGOs
Media	Television, radio, digital media and publishing companies
Public Sector	Government agencies, state corporations and regulatory authorities

You do not need hundreds of companies.

A focused list of 30–50 organisations is usually easier to manage than searching the entire internet every day.

How to Find Official Careers Pages

Most employers include links such as:

Careers
Jobs
Careers at [Company Name]
Work With Us

These links are usually found:

At the bottom of the homepage
Inside the About Us section
Within the website navigation menu

Bookmark each careers page in your browser so you can check them quickly every week instead of searching again from scratch.

Organising bookmarks into folders such as Technology, Banking, NGOs and Government can save considerable time over several months of searching.

Learn to Recognise Fake Recruitment Pages

Unfortunately, fake recruitment websites and social media pages occasionally appear online.

Before submitting personal information:

Confirm the website address matches the organisation's official domain.
Be cautious of pages asking for payment to apply.
Verify announcements through the company's verified social media accounts if unsure.
Never send money for an internship application.

Legitimate employers do not guarantee internships in exchange for payment.

How Often Should You Check?

Many organisations do not publish internships on a fixed schedule.

Checking career pages once every week is usually sufficient.

If an employer is running a major graduate recruitment programme, you may wish to check twice a week during the application period.

Pro Tip

Create a browser folder called Internship Search containing all your favourite employer career pages. Opening the folder once a week takes only a few minutes and ensures you never rely on memory alone.

## University Career Offices

Many students overlook one of the most valuable internship resources already available to them.

Most universities, colleges and TVET institutions in Kenya have career offices or student placement departments that work directly with employers. These offices often receive internship opportunities before they become public because organisations prefer recruiting students through institutions they already know.

Unfortunately, many students only visit the career office when they need a recommendation letter or after struggling to find an attachment.

That is a missed opportunity.

### What University Career Offices Actually Do

Depending on your institution, a career office may help you:

- Share internship and attachment opportunities
- Organise employer presentations
- Host career fairs
- Connect students with alumni
- Review CVs and cover letters
- Conduct mock interviews
- Verify attachment requirements
- Introduce students to graduate recruitment programmes

Some universities also maintain mailing lists where internship opportunities are sent directly to students.

If your institution offers this service, subscribe as early as possible.

### Questions to Ask Your Career Office

Instead of simply asking:

> "Do you have internships?"

Ask more specific questions.

For example:

- Which employers regularly recruit students from our department?
- Which companies recruited here last year?
- Are there employers currently requesting interns?
- Are there upcoming career fairs?
- Can someone review my CV before I apply?
- Are there alumni working in my target industry?

Specific questions usually lead to more useful advice.

### Take Advantage of Career Fairs

Career fairs allow you to meet recruiters before submitting an application.

Prepare beforehand by:

1. Researching attending organisations.
2. Printing several copies of your CV.
3. Preparing a short personal introduction.
4. Dressing professionally.
5. Asking thoughtful questions about internship opportunities.

Many recruiters remember students who demonstrate genuine interest rather than those who simply collect brochures.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Students who introduce themselves professionally at career fairs often become familiar names when applications are later reviewed.

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn has become one of the most effective platforms for finding internships in Kenya.

It is no longer just an online CV.

Recruiters actively search LinkedIn for students with relevant skills, while many employers publish internship opportunities there before advertising elsewhere.

If your profile is incomplete, recruiters may never discover you.

### How Recruiters Search for Candidates

Recruiters rarely search using someone's name.

Instead, they search using keywords.

For example:

- Finance Student
- Accounting
- Software Developer
- Civil Engineering
- Data Analysis
- Marketing
- Human Resource
- Procurement
- Graphic Design
- GIS
- Public Health

If these keywords do not appear naturally in your profile, you are less likely to appear in recruiter searches.

### Optimise Your Headline

Many students leave their headline as:

> Student at XYZ University

A stronger headline explains your area of interest.

For example:

- Computer Science Student | Interested in Software Development
- Finance Student | Excel | Financial Analysis
- Mechanical Engineering Student | CAD | Manufacturing
- Marketing Student | Digital Marketing | Content Creation

Your headline is one of the first things recruiters see.

### Complete Every Important Section

A strong LinkedIn profile should include:

- Professional profile photo
- Clear headline
- About section
- Education
- Projects
- Skills
- Volunteer experience
- Certifications
- Languages
- Contact information

Even if you have never worked before, projects and volunteer work demonstrate practical experience.

### Use Keywords Naturally

Include skills you've genuinely learned through:

- Coursework
- Research projects
- Student leadership
- Hackathons
- Volunteering
- Personal projects

Avoid adding skills you cannot discuss during an interview.

### Turn on Job Alerts

LinkedIn allows you to receive notifications when new internship opportunities are posted.

Create alerts for terms such as:

- Internship Kenya
- Graduate Internship
- Industrial Attachment
- Graduate Trainee
- Software Internship Kenya
- Finance Internship Nairobi

Receiving alerts early gives you more time to prepare quality applications.

### Easy Apply or Company Website?

Some LinkedIn vacancies include an **Easy Apply** option.

Others direct you to the employer's recruitment portal.

Whenever possible:

1. Read the full job description.
2. Visit the company's official careers page.
3. Follow all application instructions carefully.

Some employers only review applications submitted through their own recruitment systems, even if the opportunity appears on LinkedIn.

### Common LinkedIn Mistakes

Avoid these common errors:

- Incomplete profile
- No profile photo
- Misspelled words
- Outdated contact details
- Missing skills
- No projects
- Ignoring recruiter messages
- Copying someone else's profile summary

> **Pro Tip**
>
> Spend 15–20 minutes improving your LinkedIn profile every month. Small updates over time often make a bigger difference than rebuilding your profile only when you need an internship.

## Networking

Many internship opportunities are never advertised publicly.

Instead, students hear about them through lecturers, alumni, professional associations, former interns or employees already working within an organisation.

Networking is not about asking strangers for jobs.

It is about building genuine professional relationships over time.

### Start with People You Already Know

You probably have a larger network than you realise.

Think about:

- Lecturers
- Academic supervisors
- Alumni
- Class representatives
- Student leaders
- Former attachment supervisors
- Friends who recently completed internships
- Family members working in professional fields

These conversations often lead to useful information about organisations that regularly recruit interns.

### Attend Career Events

Many universities organise:

- Career fairs
- Employer information sessions
- Industry talks
- Graduate recruitment events

These events allow you to meet recruiters face-to-face.

Rather than immediately asking for an internship, introduce yourself and ask questions such as:

- What qualities do you look for in interns?
- When does your internship recruitment usually open?
- Which skills help students stand out?

Professional conversations are more memorable than simply handing over a CV.

### Join Professional Student Organisations

Many industries have active student communities.

Examples include:

- IEEE Student Branches
- Computer Society chapters
- Rotaract clubs
- AIESEC
- Engineering student associations
- Accounting and finance societies
- Law student associations
- Marketing clubs
- Entrepreneurship hubs

These organisations frequently organise employer talks, networking sessions, competitions and workshops where students interact with industry professionals.

### Learn from Alumni

University alumni often understand exactly where current students struggle because they were in the same position a few years earlier.

Reach out politely through:

- LinkedIn
- Alumni associations
- University networking events

Instead of asking for referrals immediately, ask about:

- Their internship experience
- Skills employers valued
- Recruitment timelines
- Advice for current students

Many alumni are willing to help students who demonstrate initiative.

### Follow Up Professionally

Networking does not end after the first conversation.

After meeting someone:

- Send a thank-you message.
- Connect on LinkedIn.
- Keep your message brief.
- Stay professional.
- Share occasional updates about your progress.

Strong professional relationships develop gradually.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Recruiters often remember students who ask thoughtful questions and follow up professionally. Even when there is no immediate opportunity, a positive first impression can be valuable during future recruitment rounds.
## Cold Email Strategy

Not every organisation advertises internships.

Small businesses, startups, consulting firms, research organisations and some NGOs may be willing to host interns even when they have not published an official vacancy.

A well-written cold email can help you uncover these hidden opportunities.

Cold emails are most effective when they are personalised, professional and sent to organisations where your skills genuinely match their work.

### When Cold Emails Work

Cold emails have a higher chance of success when:

- The organisation is growing.
- The company regularly works with students or graduates.
- You are applying to a specialised department.
- You have researched the organisation beforehand.
- You explain how your skills can contribute.

For example, a software development student could email a small technology company with links to personal projects or a GitHub portfolio. A marketing student might highlight social media campaigns completed during university.

The goal is not to ask for a favour but to demonstrate potential value.

### When Cold Emails Are Less Effective

Cold emailing is usually less effective when:

- The organisation has an active internship application portal.
- The internship has already been advertised publicly.
- You send the same message to dozens of companies.
- You have not researched the organisation.

If a company has clear application instructions, always follow those first.

### Writing an Effective Subject Line

Recruiters often decide whether to open an email based on the subject line.

Simple, professional examples include:

- Internship Application – Finance Student
- Industrial Attachment Request – Mechanical Engineering
- Software Development Internship Inquiry
- Marketing Internship Application – July Intake

Avoid vague subjects such as:

- Hello
- Internship
- Please Help
- Urgent

### Keep Your Email Concise

A strong cold email should briefly explain:

- Who you are
- What you are studying
- Why you are interested in the organisation
- What relevant skills or projects you have
- When you are available

Attach only the documents requested or commonly expected, such as:

- CV
- Cover letter (where appropriate)
- Portfolio (if relevant)

Large numbers of unnecessary attachments can discourage recruiters from opening your email.

### Following Up Professionally

If you receive no response, wait approximately **7–14 days** before sending one polite follow-up email.

Keep it short.

Confirm your interest, thank the recruiter for their time and ask whether additional information would be helpful.

If there is still no response after your follow-up, move on and continue applying elsewhere.

Persistence is important, but repeated emails can appear unprofessional.

### Cold Email Etiquette

Always remember to:

- Address the correct person if possible.
- Check spelling before sending.
- Use a professional email signature.
- Avoid demanding immediate responses.
- Never send the same generic message to hundreds of organisations.

> **Pro Tip**
>
> Before sending any cold email, ask yourself:
>
> *Could this message have been sent to any company?*
>
> If the answer is yes, personalise it further.

## Creating Your Weekly Internship Routine

Finding an internship is easier when it becomes a habit rather than a last-minute activity.

Instead of spending one weekend applying for dozens of vacancies, set aside a small amount of time each week for specific tasks.

A consistent routine helps you discover opportunities early, submit better applications and avoid missing deadlines.

### Monday – Review Company Career Pages

Begin the week by checking the official careers pages of companies on your target list.

Look for:

- New internship openings
- Graduate trainee programmes
- Industrial attachment opportunities
- Closing dates

Update your application tracker with any new opportunities you find.

### Tuesday – Search LinkedIn

Spend time on LinkedIn by:

- Reviewing new internship listings
- Responding to recruiter messages
- Updating your profile
- Checking Job Alerts
- Following employers in your preferred industry

Save interesting opportunities even if you plan to apply later in the week.

### Wednesday – Check JoinNexiva

Visit JoinNexiva to explore:

- Newly published internships
- Graduate opportunities
- Career advice
- Application guides

Reading one career article each week can gradually improve your CV, interview skills and job search strategy.

### Thursday – Build Your Professional Network

Dedicate time to networking.

You could:

- Attend a university career event.
- Join an online webinar.
- Connect with an alumnus on LinkedIn.
- Participate in a professional association meeting.
- Reach out to a lecturer for career advice.

Networking is most effective when done consistently rather than only when you urgently need an internship.

### Friday – Submit Applications

Reserve one day each week for sending applications.

Before clicking **Submit**, confirm that you have:

- Tailored your CV.
- Reviewed your cover letter.
- Used the correct file names.
- Attached all required documents.
- Checked for spelling errors.

Submitting fewer high-quality applications is usually more effective than sending many rushed ones.

### Weekend – Improve Your Profile

Use the weekend to strengthen your future applications.

Consider:

- Learning a new technical skill.
- Updating your CV.
- Completing an online course.
- Improving your LinkedIn profile.
- Building your portfolio.
- Reading career resources.

Every improvement increases your competitiveness for future opportunities.

### Example Weekly Internship Schedule

| Day | Primary Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Company career pages |
| Tuesday | LinkedIn search and Job Alerts |
| Wednesday | JoinNexiva opportunities and career resources |
| Thursday | Networking and professional events |
| Friday | Submit tailored applications |
| Saturday | CV improvements and learning |
| Sunday | Plan the coming week's applications |

> **Decision Framework**
>
> If you only have **30 minutes per day**, focus on one high-impact activity instead of trying to do everything at once.
>
> Small, consistent actions over several months usually produce better results than occasional bursts of intensive job searching.

## Common Mistakes

Even strong students lose internship opportunities because of avoidable mistakes.

Recruiters often notice the same patterns during every recruitment cycle.

Avoiding these mistakes can improve your chances without adding new qualifications.

### Waiting Until the Last Minute

Many organisations begin recruiting interns several months before the internship starts.

If you begin searching only after your university announces attachment dates, you may have already missed many opportunities.

Start early and search consistently.

### Sending the Same CV to Every Employer

Every internship description highlights different skills.

Before applying, compare your CV against the job requirements and emphasise the most relevant:

- Coursework
- Projects
- Technical skills
- Leadership experience
- Volunteer work

Small adjustments can make your application more relevant.

### Ignoring Application Instructions

Some employers request:

- PDF documents only
- Specific file names
- Online application forms
- Particular email subject lines
- Supporting documents

Failure to follow instructions may lead to automatic rejection before your application is reviewed.

### Applying Without Researching the Employer

Recruiters often ask:

> "What do you know about our organisation?"

Students who cannot answer this simple question appear uninterested.

Before applying, spend a few minutes learning:

- What the organisation does
- Its products or services
- Its mission
- Recent projects
- The department you're applying to

### Focusing Only on Famous Companies

Well-known employers attract thousands of applications.

Do not overlook:

- Small businesses
- Growing startups
- NGOs
- Research organisations
- Consulting firms
- County government projects

Many students gain excellent practical experience in smaller organisations before moving into larger companies later in their careers.

### Giving Up After a Few Rejections

Internship searches take time.

Not receiving a response does not necessarily mean you are unqualified.

It may simply mean:

- The position received many applications.
- Internal candidates were prioritised.
- Recruitment was paused.
- The organisation changed its hiring plans.

Review your applications, improve your documents and continue applying.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Students who continuously improve their CV, LinkedIn profile and interview skills throughout their search often perform significantly better than those who repeatedly submit the same application without making changes.
## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. When Should I Start Looking for Internships in Kenya?

Ideally, begin searching **three to six months** before your intended start date. Many organisations complete recruitment well before interns report.

---

### 2. Are Internships in Kenya Paid?

It depends on the employer.

Some organisations offer salaries or stipends, while others provide transport or lunch allowances. Some internships are unpaid but still offer valuable practical experience.

Always read the internship advertisement carefully.

---

### 3. Can First-Year Students Get Internships?

Yes, although opportunities may be limited.

Many first-year students gain experience through volunteering, student leadership, community projects and short-term attachments before applying for formal internships later.

---

### 4. Can I Apply Before Finishing University?

Yes.

Most internship programmes are specifically designed for continuing students.

Some graduate internships are open only after graduation, so always confirm the eligibility requirements.

---

### 5. Should I Accept an Unpaid Internship?

Consider:

- The skills you will gain.
- The organisation's reputation.
- The quality of mentorship.
- The duration.
- Whether you can realistically afford the costs involved.

An unpaid internship that provides excellent experience may be worthwhile, but every situation is different.

---

### 6. Can I Complete More Than One Internship?

Yes.

Many employers value candidates who have gained experience across different organisations or industries, provided each internship contributed meaningful learning.

---

### 7. Do Employers Require a Minimum GPA?

Some graduate programmes specify academic requirements, while many internships do not.

Read each advertisement carefully rather than assuming every employer has the same criteria.

---

### 8. Should I Relocate for an Internship?

If relocation is financially practical and the opportunity offers strong learning experiences, it may be worth considering.

Think about:

- Accommodation costs
- Transport
- Duration
- Career benefits
- Personal circumstances

---

### 9. Are Remote Internships Available?

Yes.

Although many Kenyan internships require physical attendance, some organisations—particularly in technology, digital marketing, design and content creation—offer hybrid or fully remote opportunities.

*(Future Guide: Remote Internships)*

---

### 10. Do I Need Recommendation Letters?

Not always.

Only include recommendation letters if the employer requests them or if they genuinely strengthen your application.

---

### 11. How Long Should My Internship CV Be?

For most students and recent graduates, a **one-page CV** is usually sufficient.

Focus on relevant education, projects, skills and achievements rather than filling space with unnecessary information.

*(Future Guide: CV Templates)*

---

### 12. Can I Apply if I Changed My Course?

Yes.

Highlight the transferable skills you've developed and explain your interest in the new field where appropriate.

Employers often value adaptability and willingness to learn.

---

### 13. How Many Internships Should I Apply for Each Week?

There is no perfect number.

Aim for several **well-tailored applications** rather than submitting dozens of generic ones.

Quality consistently beats quantity.

---

### 14. What Should I Do if I Never Hear Back?

Continue searching while waiting.

Review your CV, improve your LinkedIn profile, ask for feedback from your university career office and keep applying.

Silence is common during internship recruitment.

---

### 15. Is It Acceptable to Apply for Both Internships and Graduate Trainee Programmes?

Yes, provided you meet the eligibility requirements.

Many final-year students and recent graduates apply for both to increase their opportunities.
