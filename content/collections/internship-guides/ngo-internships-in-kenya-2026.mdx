# NGO Internships in Kenya (2026): Complete Guide to Internship Opportunities in NGOs, UN Agencies & Development Organisations

For many students and recent graduates in Kenya, an internship with an NGO represents more than workplace experience. It is an opportunity to contribute to meaningful community projects while developing practical skills that can support a long-term career in development, humanitarian assistance, public health, education, environmental conservation or governance.

Kenya hosts a wide range of organisations working across local, national and international development programmes. These include Kenyan non-governmental organisations, international NGOs (INGOs), United Nations agencies, charitable foundations and development partners. Together, they create diverse internship opportunities for students from many academic backgrounds.

Competition for these opportunities can be high because many applicants are attracted by the opportunity to gain professional experience while contributing to projects that create social impact. Successful applicants usually combine relevant academic preparation with strong applications, volunteer experience where available and genuine interest in the organisation's mission.

This guide explains how NGO internships work in Kenya, where to find opportunities, what different organisations look for and how to prepare competitive applications without making assumptions about recruitment policies that differ between organisations.

## Quick Answer: How Do You Get an NGO Internship in Kenya?

A strong NGO internship application usually involves:

1. Identifying organisations that match your interests and qualifications.
2. Preparing a tailored CV and cover letter.
3. Demonstrating relevant skills through coursework, projects or volunteer work.
4. Applying through official recruitment channels.
5. Preparing carefully for interviews if shortlisted.
6. Continuing to improve your application after each opportunity.

There is no single recruitment process that applies to every NGO. Application methods, eligibility requirements and recruitment timelines vary by organisation.

## What Is an NGO Internship?

An NGO internship is a structured learning opportunity within a non-governmental or development organisation that allows students or graduates to gain practical workplace experience while contributing to programmes that address social, economic or environmental challenges.

Depending on the organisation, interns may assist with:

- Research.
- Programme implementation.
- Community engagement.
- Communications.
- Finance.
- Monitoring and evaluation.
- Administration.
- Logistics.
- Data management.

Responsibilities vary widely depending on the organisation, department and internship.

---

### NGO

A Non-Governmental Organisation (NGO) operates independently of government and typically works to address social or development challenges.

Many NGOs operate locally within Kenya.

---

### International NGO (INGO)

An International NGO works across multiple countries and often manages regional or global development programmes.

Many INGOs maintain offices in Kenya while implementing programmes across East Africa.

---

### United Nations Agencies

United Nations agencies are specialised international organisations that support global development, humanitarian assistance, health, education and other priorities.

Each agency has its own internship programmes, eligibility requirements and recruitment processes.

---

### Non-Profit Organisations

Non-profit organisations operate for public benefit rather than distributing profits to owners or shareholders.

Some NGOs fall within this broader category.

---

### Development Partners

Development partners include international organisations, foundations and institutions that support development programmes through funding, technical expertise or partnerships.

They may also offer internship opportunities depending on organisational needs.

## Volunteer Experience vs Internship Experience

Many students underestimate the value of volunteer work.

Although volunteer positions and internships serve different purposes, both can strengthen an application.

| Volunteer Experience | Internship Experience |
|----------------------|-----------------------|
| Usually community-focused | Structured workplace learning |
| Often flexible | Usually follows organisational work schedules |
| Demonstrates commitment | Demonstrates professional readiness |
| May involve informal supervision | Usually includes workplace supervision |
| Builds practical skills | Builds practical and professional skills |

Volunteer experience can be particularly valuable when you have little or no formal work experience.

> **Career Coach Tip**
>
> Don't leave volunteer work off your CV simply because it wasn't paid. If it helped you develop relevant skills or responsibilities, it may strengthen your internship application.

## Common Mistakes When Applying for NGO Internships

Competition is often strong, so avoid mistakes that are entirely within your control.

### 1. Applying Without Research

**Why it happens**

Applicants focus only on securing any internship.

**Why it hurts**

Recruiters often want applicants who understand the organisation's mission.

**Better approach**

Read about:

- The organisation's programmes.
- Areas of work.
- Current projects.
- Values.

---

### 2. Sending Generic Applications

**Why it happens**

Applicants reuse the same CV and cover letter.

**Why it hurts**

Generic applications rarely demonstrate genuine interest.

**Better approach**

Tailor every application.

---

### 3. Ignoring Volunteer Experience

Many students believe volunteer work has little value.

In reality, relevant volunteer experience can demonstrate:

- Initiative.
- Leadership.
- Community engagement.
- Teamwork.
- Communication.

---

### 4. Focusing Only on International NGOs

International organisations are highly competitive.

Kenyan NGOs also provide valuable professional experience and meaningful development work.

Remain open to both.

---

### 5. Applying Too Late

Many students begin searching only after examinations or graduation.

Starting earlier provides more time to prepare stronger applications.

---

## Mistakes vs Better Practices

| Mistake | Better Practice |
|----------|-----------------|
| Generic CV | Tailor every application |
| No organisation research | Learn about programmes and values |
| Ignoring volunteer work | Highlight relevant community experience |
| Waiting until deadlines | Begin searching early |
| Applying randomly | Focus on organisations aligned with your interests |
| Weak cover letter | Explain genuine motivation |
| No interview preparation | Research likely interview topics |

## NGO Internship Preparation Timeline

Planning ahead reduces unnecessary pressure.

| Timeline | Recommended Action |
|----------|--------------------|
| 2–3 months before | Research organisations and identify areas of interest |
| 1 month before | Update your CV and cover letter |
