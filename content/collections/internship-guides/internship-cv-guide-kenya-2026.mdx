# Internship CV Guide (2026): How to Write a CV That Gets Internship Interviews in Kenya

Many internship applications are rejected before the recruiter reaches the second page of the CV—or before they finish reading the first one.

This doesn't necessarily happen because the applicant lacks potential. More often, it happens because the CV fails to communicate that potential quickly and clearly.

Recruiters frequently review many internship applications within a limited time. A CV that is difficult to read, poorly organised or filled with generic information may be overlooked even if the candidate has relevant skills.

The good news is that students, recent graduates and industrial attachment applicants do **not** need years of work experience to create a strong internship CV. Academic projects, leadership roles, volunteer work, coursework and practical training can all demonstrate the qualities employers value.

This guide will show you how to build a professional internship CV from scratch, understand what recruiters look for, improve your chances of being shortlisted and avoid the mistakes that commonly weaken student applications.

## Quick Answer: How Do You Write a Good Internship CV in Kenya?

A strong internship CV should:

1. Be clear and easy to read.
2. Match the internship you are applying for.
3. Highlight relevant education and skills.
4. Include projects, leadership and volunteer experience where appropriate.
5. Be free from spelling and formatting errors.
6. Demonstrate learning potential rather than exaggerating experience.
7. Follow the employer's application instructions, including the requested file format.

Your CV should make it easy for a recruiter to understand why you are a suitable internship candidate within the first few moments of reading it.

## What Is an Internship CV?

An internship CV is a professional document that introduces your education, skills, achievements and relevant experiences to a potential employer.

Unlike a CV prepared by an experienced professional, an internship CV is designed to demonstrate **potential** rather than an extensive employment history.

For many students, the CV answers one important question:

> Why should this organisation interview me despite my limited professional experience?

---

Unlike an experienced professional's CV, an internship CV places greater emphasis on:

- Education
- Academic achievements
- Projects
- Technical skills
- Leadership
- Volunteer work
- Practical training
- Learning potential

For students and recent graduates, these experiences often provide stronger evidence of future success than a long employment history.

---

### Internship CV vs Experienced Professional CV

| Internship CV | Experienced Professional CV |
|---------------|-----------------------------|
| Focuses on education and potential | Focuses on professional achievements |
| Highlights projects and coursework | Highlights employment history |
| Demonstrates transferable skills | Demonstrates industry expertise |
| Often has limited work experience | Usually includes several years of experience |
| Emphasises willingness to learn | Emphasises proven performance |

Both CVs follow similar principles of clarity and professionalism, but the content reflects different career stages.

---

### CV vs Résumé

The terms **CV** and **résumé** are sometimes used interchangeably.

In Kenya, employers commonly use the term **CV** for internship and graduate applications.

A résumé is often shorter and more concise in some international contexts, while a CV may provide more detail.

For internship applications in Kenya, follow the terminology and instructions used by the employer.

## What Recruiters Look For

Recruiters understand that internship applicants usually have limited experience.

Instead of searching for extensive employment histories, they often evaluate whether an applicant demonstrates the potential to succeed.

Common areas of focus include:

### Relevance

Does the CV relate to the internship?

Relevant coursework, projects and technical skills are often more valuable than unrelated information.

---

### Readability

Recruiters appreciate CVs that are:

- Well organised
- Easy to skim
- Clearly formatted
- Free from clutter

Good formatting helps important information stand out.

---

### Accuracy

Every detail should be truthful.

Avoid:

- Exaggerated achievements
- Skills you cannot demonstrate
- Incorrect dates
- False qualifications

Professional credibility begins with honesty.

---

### Tailoring

Recruiters often notice when applicants submit identical CVs for every opportunity.

Small adjustments that highlight relevant skills can make your application stronger.

---

### Professional Presentation

Presentation matters.

A clean, consistent CV suggests that the applicant pays attention to detail.

---

### Learning Potential

Internship recruiters frequently ask:

- Does this student show initiative?
- Are they eager to learn?
- Can they communicate professionally?
- Will they fit into the team?

These qualities often matter more than lengthy employment histories.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Internship recruiters rarely expect candidates to know everything. They are often looking for applicants who demonstrate curiosity, professionalism and the ability to learn quickly.

## The Anatomy of a Strong Internship CV

Every section of your CV serves a purpose.

Understanding what belongs in each section helps you avoid unnecessary information while highlighting your strengths.

### Contact Information

Include:

- Full name
- Professional phone number
- Professional email address
- LinkedIn profile (if maintained)
- Portfolio or GitHub (where relevant)

Avoid:

- Nicknames
- Unprofessional email addresses
- Multiple phone numbers unless necessary

---

### Professional Summary

A professional summary is a short introduction placed near the top of your CV.

It should briefly explain:

- Your academic background.
- Your career interests.
- Your strongest relevant skills.
- The value you hope to bring.

Keep it concise.

---

### Education

For many internship applicants, education is one of the strongest sections.

Include:

- Institution
- Qualification
- Area of study
- Graduation or expected graduation date

You may also include notable academic achievements where relevant.

---

### Skills

List skills that genuinely relate to the internship.

Examples include:

- Microsoft Excel
- Programming languages
- Data analysis
- Report writing
- Communication
- Research
- Teamwork
- Presentation skills

Avoid listing every skill you have ever encountered.

---

### Academic Projects

Projects demonstrate how you applied classroom learning.

Describe:

- Your objective.
- Your role.
- The skills used.
- The outcome.

Specific examples strengthen credibility.

---

### Work Experience

If you have previous employment, internships or industrial attachment experience, include:

- Organisation
- Position

- Achievements

Focus on relevance rather than length.

---

### Volunteer Experience

Volunteer work can demonstrate:

It is especially valuable for applicants with limited professional experience.

---

### Leadership Experience

Leadership can come from:

- Student associations
- Clubs

Explain your responsibilities and contributions rather than simply listing titles.

### Certifications

Relevant certifications may strengthen your CV.

Examples include:

## How to Write Each Section

Understanding the purpose of each section is only the first step.

The next step is writing each one effectively.

### Writing a Strong Professional Summary

Your summary should answer three questions:

1. Who are you?
2. What are you studying or qualified in?
3. Why are you applying?

A good summary is specific, concise and relevant.

Poor example:

> Hardworking student seeking opportunities.

Stronger example:

> Final-year Information Technology student with practical experience gained through software development projects and student leadership. Interested in applying technical and problem-solving skills through an internship while continuing to learn from experienced professionals.

Notice how the stronger version provides meaningful information rather than generic claims.

---

### Presenting Your Education

List your most recent qualification first.

Where appropriate, include:

- Institution
- Qualification
- Area of study
- Graduation date or expected completion

Avoid unnecessary detail such as listing every subject unless specifically requested.

> **Career Coach Tip**
>
> Arrange your CV so that your strongest qualifications appear first. For most internship applicants, education, projects and relevant skills deserve greater prominence than unrelated work experience.
### Describing Your Skills

Instead of creating a long list of unrelated skills, focus on those that are relevant to the internship.

For example, if you're applying for a finance internship, prioritise skills such as:

- Microsoft Excel
- Financial analysis
- Report writing
- Data interpretation
- Attention to detail

For an IT internship, you might instead highlight:

- Programming languages
- Database management
- Networking fundamentals
- Version control
- Problem-solving

Tailoring your skills section helps recruiters quickly see your relevance.

---

### Writing Better Project Descriptions

Many students simply write:

> Final Year Project

without explaining what they actually did.

Instead, describe:

- The objective.
- Your responsibilities.
- The technologies or methods used.
- The outcome.

Example:

**Inventory Management System**

- Developed a web-based inventory management system as part of a university group project.
- Coordinated database design and testing.
- Improved teamwork, communication and problem-solving skills while meeting project deadlines.

This approach demonstrates practical application rather than simply listing a project title.

---

### Presenting Volunteer and Leadership Experience

Recruiters often value initiative.

Volunteer work and leadership positions demonstrate:

- Responsibility.
- Communication.
- Organisation.
- Teamwork.
- Time management.

Describe your contribution rather than simply naming the organisation.

## ATS-Friendly CV Guide

Many students worry that every employer uses an Applicant Tracking System (ATS).

The reality is more nuanced.

Some organisations use ATS software during recruitment, while others review CVs manually.

Practices vary by employer and organisation size.

Rather than trying to "beat the ATS," focus on producing a clear, professional CV that is easy for both people and software to read.

---

### What Is an ATS?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software that helps some employers organise and review applications.

Depending on how an organisation recruits, an ATS may:

- Store applications.
- Search for keywords.
- Filter applications.
- Assist recruiters during shortlisting.

Not every internship recruitment process uses ATS software.

---

### ATS-Friendly Formatting

Simple formatting generally works best.

Recommended practices include:

- Clear section headings.
- Standard fonts.
- Consistent spacing.
- Simple bullet points.
- Logical structure.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

---

### Keyword Matching

Review the internship advertisement carefully.

Notice the language used to describe:

- Skills.
- Qualifications.
- Responsibilities.

Where appropriate, naturally reflect relevant terminology in your CV.

Do not copy entire job descriptions.

---

### Fonts

Choose professional, readable fonts such as:

- Calibri
- Arial
- Aptos
- Times New Roman

Avoid decorative fonts that reduce readability.

---

### Tables and Graphics

Simple layouts are usually easier to read.

Avoid placing essential information inside:

- Graphics
- Icons
- Text boxes
- Complex tables

These elements may reduce readability for some systems and readers.

---

### PDF or Word?

Always follow the employer's instructions.

Some employers request:

- PDF
- Microsoft Word
- Online application forms

Do not assume one format is universally preferred.

---

## ATS Myths vs Reality

Many online articles exaggerate ATS requirements.

The table below separates common myths from more balanced guidance.

| Myth | Reality |
|------|----------|
| Every employer uses ATS. | Recruitment methods vary between employers. |
| Fancy designs always prevent shortlisting. | Clear, readable formatting is generally more important than elaborate design. |
| You should copy every keyword from the job advert. | Use relevant terminology naturally rather than copying entire descriptions. |
| ATS alone decides who gets interviewed. | Many organisations combine software tools with human review. |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Should my internship CV be one page?

For many students and recent graduates, a concise one-page CV is often sufficient.

If you have additional relevant experience, a second page may be appropriate, but avoid adding unnecessary information simply to increase the length.

---

### 2. Can I include my KCSE education?

If you are currently studying at a university, TVET institution or college, your current qualification should usually receive greater emphasis.

Whether to include earlier education depends on your experience and the employer's requirements.

---

### 3. Should I include my photograph?

Some employers request photographs, while many do not.

Follow the employer's application instructions rather than assuming a photo is required.

---

### 4. Should I include referees?

Practices vary.

Some employers ask applicants to list referees, while others state that references will only be requested later.

Always follow the vacancy announcement.

---

### 5. Should I submit my CV as a PDF or a Word document?

Use the format requested by the employer.

If no preference is provided, many applicants choose PDF because it helps preserve formatting, but employer instructions should always take priority.

---

### 6. Does every internship employer use an ATS?

No.

Some organisations use Applicant Tracking Systems, while others review applications manually.

A clear, well-organised CV is beneficial regardless of the recruitment method.

---

### 7. Should I include my GPA?

Include your GPA only if it strengthens your application or the employer specifically requests it.

If it is not requested and does not add meaningful value, it may not be necessary.

---

### 8. Can I create a good CV without work experience?

Yes.

You can demonstrate your potential through:

- Coursework
- Academic projects
- Leadership
- Volunteer work
- Competitions
- Industrial attachment
- Technical skills

Recruiters hiring interns generally understand that students are still building professional experience.

---

### 9. Should I include my LinkedIn profile?

If your LinkedIn profile is complete, professional and consistent with your CV, including it can strengthen your application.

If it is incomplete or outdated, update it before adding it to your CV.

---

### 10. Should I list hobbies?

Only include hobbies if they contribute positively to your application or demonstrate relevant interests and skills.

Avoid filling space with unrelated information.

---

### 11. How should I present soft skills?

Instead of listing soft skills without evidence, demonstrate them through projects, leadership roles and achievements.

Specific examples are more persuasive than generic claims.

---

### 12. How often should I update my CV?

Review your CV whenever you gain:

- A new certification.
- A new project.
- Leadership experience.
- Volunteer work.
- Technical skills.
- Internship experience.

Keeping your CV current makes future applications easier.

---

### 13. Should I use AI to write my CV?

AI tools can help you organise ideas, improve grammar or draft sections of your CV.

However, recruiters can often recognise generic, unedited content.

Always review, personalise and verify your CV so it accurately reflects your own education, experiences and skills.
