## Why Choosing the Right Internship Website Matters

Not every internship website serves the same purpose.

Some specialise in graduate programmes.

Others focus on entry-level jobs.

Some aggregate vacancies from multiple employers, while others publish opportunities directly from organisations.

Understanding these differences helps you spend your time more effectively.

Imagine two students looking for a finance internship.

The first spends every day refreshing a single general job board.

The second checks:

- LinkedIn Jobs
- Company career pages
- JoinNexiva
- Their university career office
- Two employer newsletters

Although both students invest similar amounts of time, the second student is likely to discover more relevant opportunities because they are searching across multiple trusted sources.

### Why employers use different recruitment channels

There is no standard recruitment process followed by every employer.

Organisations choose platforms based on their hiring needs.

For example:

| Employer Type | Common Recruitment Channels |
| --- | --- |
| Banks | Company career pages, LinkedIn, graduate recruitment portals |
| Technology companies | LinkedIn, company websites, developer communities |
| NGOs | ReliefWeb, organisation websites, development job boards |
| Government agencies | Public Service Commission and official government portals |
| Universities | Institutional career offices and notice boards |
| SMEs | LinkedIn, referrals and direct applications |

Because recruitment methods vary, students who rely on only one website often miss excellent opportunities.

### Why one website is never enough

Each internship platform has strengths and limitations.

For example:

- Some publish opportunities quickly but receive thousands of applications.
- Others list fewer internships but attract less competition.
- Some specialise in technology roles.
- Others focus on graduate trainee programmes or humanitarian organisations.

Using multiple trusted platforms gives you broader coverage without relying on chance.

> **Pro Tip**
>
> Instead of checking one website every day, create a shortlist of trusted internship platforms and review each one on a consistent weekly schedule. This approach is more effective than repeatedly refreshing a single job board.

## Where Employers Recruit Before Posting Publicly

One of the biggest misconceptions among students is that every internship appears on a job website.

In practice, many opportunities are shared privately before they ever reach public recruitment platforms.

Some positions are filled through existing university partnerships, employee referrals or professional networks without being widely advertised.

Understanding these recruitment channels can give you a valuable head start.

### University Departments

Many employers contact university departments directly when looking for interns.

Departments may receive internship requests from organisations that have previously recruited their students successfully.

For this reason, regularly checking departmental notice boards, student email lists and faculty announcements can uncover opportunities that never appear online.

### Career Fairs

Career fairs often provide access to internship opportunities before formal applications open.

Recruiters may:

- Announce upcoming internship programmes.
- Collect CVs for future recruitment.
- Encourage students to apply once vacancies are published.
- Explain the skills they look for in successful applicants.

Students who attend these events are often better prepared when recruitment officially begins.

### Alumni Networks

Graduates frequently share internship opportunities with current students before they become public.

University alumni groups on LinkedIn, WhatsApp and professional association forums can be valuable sources of early information.

Rather than asking alumni for jobs, ask about:

- Recruitment timelines.
- Internship requirements.
- Skills employers value.
- Advice for preparing competitive applications.

### LinkedIn Posts

Not every recruiter publishes vacancies through the LinkedIn Jobs section.

Some hiring managers announce internship opportunities directly on their personal LinkedIn profiles.

Following recruiters, HR professionals and company pages increases your chances of seeing these announcements early.

### Employee Referrals

Many organisations encourage employees to recommend suitable internship candidates.

A referral does not guarantee an internship, but it can ensure your application reaches the appropriate recruitment team more quickly.

Professional networking helps you become aware of these opportunities before they are widely advertised.

### Professional Associations

Student chapters of organisations such as IEEE, AIESEC, Rotaract and other professional associations regularly organise employer talks, workshops and networking events.

Employers sometimes mention upcoming internship programmes during these sessions before vacancies appear on company websites or job boards.

> **Pro Tip**
>
> The best internship opportunities are not always hidden—they are often shared earlier through trusted networks. Combining job websites with university channels, networking and company career pages gives you the broadest possible view of the internship market.

## Internship Scams to Avoid

Unfortunately, not every internship advertisement is genuine.

Fraudulent recruiters sometimes target students who are eager to gain work experience.

Understanding common warning signs can help you avoid losing money or sharing personal information with scammers.

### Requests for Application Fees

Legitimate employers do not require applicants to pay money simply to submit an internship application.

Be cautious if someone asks you to pay for:

- Registration fees
- Processing fees
- Interview fees
- Shortlisting fees
- Employment guarantees

Always verify opportunities through official employer websites.

---

### Fake Recruiters

Scammers sometimes impersonate recruiters using unofficial email addresses or social media accounts.

Before responding:

- Confirm the recruiter's identity.
- Visit the organisation's official website.
- Compare contact information.
- Check whether the vacancy appears on the employer's careers page.

If something seems unusual, contact the organisation directly using publicly listed contact details.

---

### WhatsApp Recruitment Groups

While some organisations share updates through messaging platforms, unsolicited WhatsApp messages promising guaranteed internships should be treated with caution.

Warning signs include:

- Requests for payment.
- Pressure to respond immediately.
- Poor grammar.
- No official application process.
- Claims of guaranteed employment.

Legitimate recruitment normally involves formal application procedures.

---

### Suspicious Email Domains

Professional organisations usually communicate using official company email domains.

For example:

- `@companyname.com`
- `@organisation.org`

Be cautious of messages sent from unrelated free email addresses claiming to represent major employers.

Always verify before sharing personal documents.

---

### Requests for Personal Financial Information

You should never be asked to provide:

- Mobile money payments
- Bank PINs
- ATM details
- Online banking passwords

Recruitment processes do not require this information.

---

### Verify Before You Apply

Before submitting an application, ask yourself:

- Does the vacancy appear on the employer's official website?
- Is the email address legitimate?
- Are the application instructions professional?
- Has anyone asked for payment?
- Does the opportunity sound realistic?

If several warning signs appear together, avoid the opportunity and continue your search elsewhere.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Genuine employers compete for talented candidates. They do not ask applicants to pay for internships or guarantee employment before completing a formal recruitment process.

### 6. Should I apply even if I don't meet every requirement?

If you meet most of the essential requirements and can demonstrate a willingness to learn, it may still be worth applying.

Avoid applying when you clearly do not meet mandatory eligibility criteria such as course requirements or graduation status.

### 7. How many internship websites should I check?

Aim to monitor at least four or five reliable sources each week.

A combination of JoinNexiva, LinkedIn Jobs, company career pages, your university career office and one or two additional job platforms provides broad coverage without becoming overwhelming.

### 8. Do employers prefer applications through LinkedIn or their own website?

If an employer directs applicants to its official careers page, follow those instructions.

Many organisations only review applications submitted through their own recruitment system, even if the vacancy was first discovered on LinkedIn.

### 9. Are paid internships better than unpaid internships?

Not necessarily.

A paid internship can provide financial support, but the quality of mentorship, practical experience and career development opportunities are often more important when evaluating an internship.

Yes.

### 11. Are international internship websites useful for Kenyan students?

Yes.

Platforms such as UN Careers and ReliefWeb advertise opportunities within international organisations, humanitarian agencies and development programmes that may be relevant to Kenyan students and graduates.

### 12. What should I do if I cannot find internships in my field?

Expand your search strategy.

In addition to job websites, check company career pages, speak with your university career office, attend career fairs, network with alumni and consider sending carefully researched cold emails to suitable organisations.

---

### 13. How long should I keep applying?

Continue applying until you secure an internship.

Many students submit several quality applications before receiving interviews. Consistency and continuous improvement usually produce better results than submitting a large number of rushed applications.

### 14. Should I create a LinkedIn profile even if I have no work experience?

Yes.

Recruiters understand that students may have limited professional experience.

Include your education, academic projects, technical skills, volunteer work, certifications and student leadership roles to build a strong profile.
