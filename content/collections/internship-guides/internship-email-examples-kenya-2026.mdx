# Internship Email Examples in Kenya (2026): Professional Email Templates for Every Stage of Your Internship Application

Your email is often the first interaction an employer has with you.

Before a recruiter opens your CV, reads your cover letter or invites you for an interview, they may first see your email subject line, your sender name and the professionalism of your message.

A strong application can lose impact if it is accompanied by an unclear subject line, missing attachments or an unprofessional email.

Fortunately, writing professional internship emails is a skill that anyone can learn.

This guide provides practical email templates for every stage of the internship journey—from your first application to follow-up messages, interview thank-you emails and accepting internship offers.

You'll also learn how recruiters evaluate emails, how to avoid common mistakes and how to adapt templates without sounding generic.

## Quick Answer: How Do You Write a Professional Internship Application Email?

A professional internship email should:

1. Use a professional email address.
2. Include a clear subject line.
3. Address the correct recipient.
4. Introduce yourself briefly.
5. Explain why you are writing.
6. Mention the attached documents.
7. Close professionally.
8. Be proofread before sending.

Your email should complement your CV and cover letter—not repeat them.

## Why Email Still Matters

Not every employer accepts internship applications by email.

Some organisations use online application portals, recruitment systems or dedicated career websites instead.

However, many employers in Kenya still request applications by email or communicate with shortlisted candidates through email.

That makes professional email communication an essential skill.

A clear, well-written email demonstrates:

- Professionalism.
- Attention to detail.
- Communication skills.
- Respect for the recipient's time.

An email should support the quality of your application—not undermine it.

> **Recruiter Insight**
>
> Recruiters often decide whether to open an application within seconds of seeing the sender name, subject line and overall presentation of the email.

## Professional Email Anatomy

Every professional internship email contains several important parts.

### Professional Email Address

Your email address should be simple and professional.

Good examples:

- jane.wanjiku@email.com
- brian.otieno@email.com

Avoid:

- coolboy254@email.com
- queenbee123@email.com
- footballking@email.com

---

### Subject Line

The subject line tells the recruiter why you are writing before they open the message.

Keep it concise and specific.

---

### Greeting

Use a professional greeting.

Examples include:

- Dear Hiring Manager,
- Dear Recruitment Team,
- Dear Human Resources Team,

If the advertisement names a contact person, address them appropriately.

---

### Opening Paragraph

Introduce yourself and explain why you are writing.

Keep this section brief.

---

### Main Message

Mention:

- The internship.
- Your qualification.
- The documents attached.

Avoid repeating your entire cover letter.

---

### Closing

Thank the recipient for their time and consideration.

Express your interest professionally.

---

### Signature

Include:

- Full name.
- Phone number.
- Professional email address.

A simple signature is usually sufficient.
## Complete Internship Application Email Example

The example below uses a fictional student and can be adapted to different internship opportunities.

**Subject**

```text
Application for Finance Internship – Grace Wanjiku
```

**Email Body**

```text
Dear Hiring Manager,

I hope you are doing well.

I am writing to apply for the Finance Internship advertised by your organisation.

I am currently a final-year Bachelor of Commerce (Finance) student with an interest in financial analysis, reporting and business operations. I believe this internship would provide an excellent opportunity to apply my academic knowledge while learning from experienced professionals.

Please find attached my CV and cover letter for your consideration.

Thank you for taking the time to review my application. I would appreciate the opportunity to discuss my suitability for the internship during an interview.

Kind regards,

Grace Wanjiku
0712 345 678
gracewanjiku@email.com
```

**Attachments**

- Grace_Wanjiku_CV.pdf
- Grace_Wanjiku_Cover_Letter.pdf

This email is intentionally brief.

The detailed information belongs in your attached CV and cover letter.

## Industrial Attachment Email Example

Industrial attachment requests are similar to internship application emails but often emphasise your academic programme and practical training requirements.

**Subject**

```text
Application for Industrial Attachment – Mechanical Engineering Student
```

**Email Body**

```text
Dear Recruitment Team,

I am writing to request consideration for an industrial attachment opportunity within your organisation.

I am currently pursuing a Diploma in Mechanical Engineering and am seeking an attachment opportunity that will allow me to apply the practical skills and technical knowledge gained during my studies.

Please find attached my CV and cover letter for your review.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Kind regards,

Kevin Mwangi
0711 234 567
kevinmwangi@email.com
```

The main difference is that industrial attachment applications often highlight academic practical training requirements rather than graduate career development.

## Follow-Up Email Example

Sometimes applicants wonder whether they should follow up after submitting an application.

There is no universal rule.

Some employers welcome polite follow-up messages, while others clearly state that only shortlisted candidates will be contacted.

Always respect any instructions provided in the vacancy announcement.

### Appropriate Follow-Up

A follow-up email should:

- Be brief.
- Remain polite.
- Avoid demanding a response.
- Refer to your earlier application.

**Subject**

```text
Follow-Up on Internship Application – Grace Wanjiku
```

**Email Body**

```text
Dear Hiring Manager,

I hope you are well.

I am writing to follow up on my application for the Finance Internship submitted on 5 July 2026.

I remain interested in the opportunity and would be grateful for any update you are able to provide regarding the recruitment process.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Kind regards,

Grace Wanjiku
```

A single professional follow-up is generally more appropriate than sending repeated reminders.

## Thank-You Email After an Interview

Not every employer expects a thank-you email after an interview.

However, where appropriate, a short, genuine message can reinforce professionalism and appreciation.

### What to Include

A good thank-you email should:

- Thank the interviewer.
- Mention the interview briefly.
- Reaffirm your interest.
- Keep the message concise.

**Subject**

```text
Thank You – Finance Internship Interview
```

**Email Body**

```text
Dear Hiring Manager,

Thank you for taking the time to meet with me today regarding the Finance Internship opportunity.

I appreciated learning more about your organisation and the internship programme. Our discussion strengthened my interest in contributing to your team while continuing to develop my skills.

Thank you once again for your time and consideration.

Kind regards,

Grace Wanjiku
```

The objective is appreciation—not repeating your entire interview.

## Cold Outreach Email Example

Occasionally, students identify organisations where they would like to intern even though no internship has been advertised.

A polite enquiry may help you learn whether internship opportunities exist.

However, there is no guarantee that the organisation will respond or have available placements.

### Cold Outreach Email

**Subject**

```text
Internship Enquiry – Information Technology Student
```

**Email Body**

```text
Dear Recruitment Team,

I hope you are doing well.

My name is Brian Otieno, and I am currently pursuing a Bachelor of Science in Information Technology.

I am writing to enquire whether your organisation offers internship opportunities for university students.

If opportunities are available, I would appreciate any information regarding the application process.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Kind regards,

Brian Otieno
0713 456 789
```

A respectful enquiry is more effective than demanding an internship opportunity.

## Accepting an Internship Offer

Receiving an internship offer is an opportunity to demonstrate professionalism.

Respond promptly where possible and express appreciation.

**Subject**

```text
Acceptance of Internship Offer – Grace Wanjiku
```

**Email Body**

```text
Dear Hiring Manager,

Thank you for offering me the Finance Internship position.

I am pleased to accept the opportunity and appreciate the confidence you have shown in me.

I look forward to joining your organisation and learning from your team.

Please let me know if there are any additional documents or information required before my start date.

Kind regards,

Grace Wanjiku
```

## Declining an Internship Offer Professionally

Sometimes you may decide to accept another opportunity instead.

Respond respectfully and thank the employer for their consideration.

**Subject**

```text
Internship Offer – Appreciation
```

**Email Body**

```text
Dear Hiring Manager,

Thank you very much for offering me the internship opportunity.

After careful consideration, I have decided to pursue another opportunity that is more closely aligned with my current goals.

I sincerely appreciate your time throughout the recruitment process and wish your organisation continued success.

Kind regards,

Grace Wanjiku
```

Professional communication helps preserve positive relationships.

## Common Internship Email Mistakes

Even a well-written CV and cover letter can be undermined by a poorly written email.

Below are some of the most common mistakes internship applicants make and how to avoid them.

---

### 1. Empty or Vague Subject Line

**Why it happens**

Applicants rush to send the email.

**Why it hurts**

Recruiters may struggle to identify the purpose of the email.

**Better approach**

Use a clear subject line that includes the internship title where appropriate.

---

### 2. Sending the Email to the Wrong Recipient

**Why it happens**

Copying previous emails or typing addresses manually.

**Why it hurts**

Your application may never reach the intended recruiter.

**Better approach**

Verify the recipient's email address before sending.

---

### 3. Forgetting Attachments

**Why it happens**

Attachments are often added after writing the email, making them easy to forget.

**Why it hurts**

An incomplete application may not be considered.

**Better approach**

Attach your documents before writing the email or use your email provider's attachment reminder if available.

---

### 4. Using Informal Language

**Why it happens**

Students write emails the same way they message friends.

**Why it hurts**

It creates an unprofessional first impression.

**Better approach**

Use polite, professional language throughout.

---

### 5. Using Emojis

**Why it happens**

Habit from social messaging platforms.

**Why it hurts**

Professional recruitment emails rarely require emojis.

**Better approach**

Keep your communication clear and professional.

---

### 6. Typographical Errors

**Why it happens**

Rushing to submit.

**Why it hurts**

Mistakes suggest poor attention to detail.

**Better approach**

Proofread every email before sending.

---

### 7. Sending Very Large Attachments

**Why it happens**

Applicants attach unnecessary files or high-resolution images.

**Why it hurts**

Large attachments may be inconvenient for recipients or exceed email limits.

**Better approach**

Only attach documents requested by the employer and optimise file sizes where appropriate.

---

### 8. Using an Unprofessional Email Address

**Why it happens**

Students continue using personal email addresses created years earlier.

**Why it hurts**

It can reduce professionalism before the recruiter even opens the message.

**Better approach**

Use a simple email address based on your name.

---

### 9. Sending the Email Without Reviewing It

**Why it happens**

Applicants focus on speed instead of accuracy.

**Why it hurts**

Simple mistakes may affect the quality of your application.

**Better approach**

Review:

- Subject line.
- Recipient.
- Attachments.
- Grammar.
- Spelling.
- Signature.

## Adapting One Email Template Without Sounding Generic

A reusable template saves time, but every application should still feel personalised.

### Usually Stays the Same

- Greeting format
- Closing
- Signature
- Overall structure

### Should Be Updated

- Employer name
- Internship title
- Reason for applying
- Skills highlighted
- Reference to the organisation

Small changes make your email feel genuinely written for the opportunity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Should I attach my cover letter?

If the employer requests a cover letter, include it with your application.

If no cover letter is requested, follow the instructions in the vacancy announcement.

---

### 2. Should I send my documents as PDF or Word?

Always follow the employer's instructions.

If a preferred format is specified, use that format.

---

### 3. Can I send internship applications from Gmail?

Yes.

The email provider is generally less important than maintaining a professional email address and writing a professional message.

---

### 4. Should I use my university email?

If it is active and professional, you may use it.

Many students also maintain a personal professional email address that remains available after graduation.

---

### 5. Should I send internship emails at night?

Practices vary.

A well-written email is generally more important than the exact time it is sent, unless the employer specifies a deadline.

---

### 6. How long should the email be?

Keep it brief.

The purpose of the email is to introduce your application, not replace your CV or cover letter.

---

### 7. Can I follow up if I receive no response?

Where appropriate, yes.

One polite follow-up message may be reasonable unless the employer has stated that they will only contact shortlisted candidates.

---

### 8. Should I CC anyone?

Only if the application instructions request it or there is a clear professional reason to do so.

Avoid copying unnecessary recipients.

---

### 9. Can AI help me write internship emails?

AI tools can help you draft professional emails or improve grammar.

However, always personalise the final version so it accurately reflects your own circumstances and the organisation you are contacting.

---

### 10. Should I send one email to multiple employers?

No.

Prepare separate, personalised emails for each employer.

Sending the same email to multiple organisations increases the risk of mistakes and appears less professional.

---

### 11. Should I include my CV in the email body?

No.

Briefly introduce yourself and mention that your CV is attached.

---

### 12. What should I do if I forget an attachment?

Send a short follow-up email as soon as you notice the mistake, apologise briefly for the omission and attach the missing document.
