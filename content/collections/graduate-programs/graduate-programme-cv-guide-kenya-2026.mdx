# Graduate Programme CV Guide in Kenya (2026): How to Get Shortlisted

Graduate programmes are among the most competitive entry points into professional employment in Kenya.

A single programme may attract hundreds or even thousands of applicants from universities across the country. Most candidates have similar academic backgrounds, limited formal work experience, and broadly comparable qualifications.

That makes your CV especially important.

It is often the first evidence a recruiter sees of your potential, professionalism, judgement, and readiness for structured early-career development.

A graduate programme CV is not simply a shorter version of an experienced professional's CV.

It must solve a different problem.

An experienced candidate can rely on years of employment history, promotions, major projects, and measurable business results. A recent graduate usually cannot. Your task is therefore to prove potential through the evidence you do have:

- education;
- internships;
- attachments;
- academic projects;
- leadership;
- volunteering;
- certifications;
- technical skills;
- extracurricular achievements;
- and examples of initiative.

This guide explains how to turn that evidence into a focused, persuasive CV designed specifically for graduate programme shortlisting in Kenya.

It will show you what recruiters look for, how to structure each section, how to tailor your CV to different employers, how to avoid common screening mistakes, and how to present limited experience without sounding weak or inexperienced.

# Quick Answer

A strong graduate programme CV should usually be:

- one to two pages long;
- tailored to the specific programme;
- easy to scan;
- focused on evidence rather than claims;
- rich in relevant keywords;
- clear about education, projects, internships, leadership, and skills;
- free from decorative design elements that reduce readability;
- and written to show potential, not apologise for limited experience.

The best graduate CVs make it easy for a recruiter to answer five questions:

1. Does this candidate meet the minimum requirements?
2. Do they understand the programme?
3. Have they demonstrated initiative?
4. Can they communicate clearly?
5. Is there evidence of leadership, learning ability, teamwork, or problem-solving?

If your CV answers those questions quickly, your chances of progressing improve significantly.

# What Makes a Graduate Programme CV Different?

A graduate programme CV is designed for structured early-career recruitment.

That usually means the employer is not hiring you for a narrowly defined entry-level role alone. They may be hiring for a programme that includes:

- departmental rotations;
- technical training;
- leadership development;
- mentorship;
- assessments;
- cross-functional assignments;
- and future management or specialist responsibilities.

The recruiter is therefore evaluating more than whether you can perform one immediate task.

They are assessing whether you have the potential to grow.

That changes what your CV must emphasise.

## A General Entry-Level CV Often Focuses On

- qualifications;
- basic skills;
- previous duties;
- and general employability.

## A Graduate Programme CV Must Also Show

- learning agility;
- leadership potential;
- curiosity;
- resilience;
- teamwork;
- analytical ability;
- communication;
- adaptability;
- commercial or organisational awareness;
- and sustained achievement.

A candidate may have very little paid experience and still produce a strong graduate programme CV if they present their evidence well.

For example, leading a university society, coordinating a class project, completing a difficult research assignment, building a portfolio, volunteering in a community initiative, or improving a process during attachment can all demonstrate valuable workplace potential.

The weakness is not limited experience.

The weakness is failing to translate that experience into evidence recruiters can understand.

# How Recruiters Screen Graduate Programme CVs

Graduate recruitment often involves high application volumes.

That means your CV may receive an initial review lasting only a few seconds before a recruiter decides whether it deserves closer attention.

The first screening stage usually checks:

- eligibility;
- degree discipline;
- graduation period;
- academic performance where required;
- location or work authorisation;
- application completeness;
- and relevant keywords.

Candidates who pass that stage may then be assessed more carefully for evidence of potential.

This is why both substance and presentation matter.

A well-qualified candidate can be overlooked if their CV is difficult to scan, poorly organised, or missing information the employer needs.

# The Graduate Shortlisting Scorecard

Use the following framework to evaluate your CV before applying.

## The PACE Scorecard

A strong graduate programme CV should show evidence across four areas:

| Area | What Recruiters Look For |
|---|---|
| **P — Potential** | Learning ability, growth, curiosity, initiative, adaptability |
| **A — Achievement** | Results, academic performance, project outcomes, measurable contributions |
| **C — Capability** | Technical skills, communication, teamwork, problem-solving |
| **E — Employer Fit** | Relevance to the programme, understanding of the organisation, aligned motivation |

A weak CV may include information in all four categories but present it vaguely.

A strong CV makes the evidence specific.

For example:

**Weak**

> Hardworking graduate with good communication and leadership skills.

**Stronger**

> Coordinated a five-member university research team, assigned responsibilities, consolidated findings, and delivered the final presentation two days before the deadline.

The stronger version shows leadership, organisation, teamwork, and execution without relying on empty adjectives.

# Recommended Length and Structure

For most graduate programme applications in Kenya, your CV should be between one and two pages.

A one-page CV may be suitable when:

- you have limited experience;
- your degree is recent;
- your projects and achievements fit comfortably;
- and the employer requests a concise application.

A two-page CV may be appropriate when:

- you have multiple internships;
- substantial leadership experience;
- relevant projects;
- certifications;
- volunteering;
- or technical portfolios.

Do not force a strong CV into one page if it becomes cramped and difficult to read.

At the same time, do not stretch limited information across two pages using large spacing or unnecessary sections.

## Recommended Order

1. Name and contact details
2. Professional summary
3. Education
4. Experience or internships
5. Projects
6. Leadership and extracurricular activities
7. Skills
8. Certifications
9. Achievements
10. Volunteering
11. Referees, if requested

The exact order may change depending on your strongest evidence.

For example:

- An engineering graduate with strong projects may place projects immediately after education.
- A business graduate with two internships may place experience earlier.
- A recent graduate with little formal experience but substantial leadership may place leadership before projects.

The strongest sections should appear where recruiters will notice them quickly.

# Writing a Strong Professional Summary

The professional summary appears near the top of the CV and should provide a quick, relevant introduction.

It should not be a generic paragraph full of adjectives.

A strong summary usually includes:

- your degree or professional identity;
- one or two relevant strengths;
- the strongest evidence you have;
- and the type of programme or contribution you are targeting.

## Weak Example

> Motivated and hardworking graduate seeking an opportunity in a reputable organisation where I can use my skills and grow my career.

This says almost nothing.

It could belong to any candidate applying for any job.

## Stronger Example

> Recent Bachelor of Commerce graduate with internship experience in financial reporting, customer service, and data analysis. Proficient in Excel and Power BI, with a record of leading university projects and presenting findings to diverse audiences. Seeking a graduate trainee programme that offers cross-functional exposure and structured professional development.

The stronger version is specific, relevant, and evidence-based.

## Summary Formula

Use this structure:

**Professional identity + relevant experience + key strengths + target contribution**

### Example for an Engineering Graduate

> Graduate mechanical engineer with industrial attachment experience in preventive maintenance, production reporting, and workplace safety. Skilled in AutoCAD, Excel, and technical documentation, with practical experience supporting maintenance teams in a manufacturing environment. Seeking a graduate engineering programme focused on operational improvement and technical development.

### Example for an ICT Graduate

> Computer science graduate with hands-on experience in web development, database design, and user support. Built and deployed three academic and personal projects using React, Node.js, and PostgreSQL. Interested in a technology graduate programme that combines software development, problem-solving, and structured mentorship.

# Education Section

For recent graduates, education is usually one of the strongest sections.

Include:

- degree title;
- institution;
- graduation year or expected graduation date;
- relevant academic performance where helpful;
- selected coursework;
- dissertation or final-year project;
- academic awards where relevant.

## Example

**Bachelor of Science in Statistics**  
Kenyatta University, Nairobi  
Graduated: December 2025  
Second Class Honours, Upper Division

Relevant coursework:

- Statistical Modelling
- Data Mining
- Econometrics
- Research Methods
- Database Management

Final-year project:

> Analysed household expenditure patterns using R and Excel, cleaned a dataset of 3,500 records, and presented findings on income distribution and food-security trends.

Do not list every subject you studied.

Choose only those relevant to the graduate programme.

## Should You Include KCSE Results?

You may include KCSE results when:

- the employer asks for them;
- the programme has minimum secondary-school requirements;
- or you are a very recent graduate with limited content.

Otherwise, university education should usually receive greater emphasis.

## Academic Performance

Include your class of degree, GPA, or grades when:

- the employer requires them;
- they strengthen your application;
- or the programme is academically competitive.

Do not hide required information.

If your academic performance is weaker, strengthen the rest of the CV with projects, skills, leadership, and practical experience.

# Internship and Attachment Experience

Internships and industrial attachments are often the most important forms of experience on a graduate CV.

Do not describe them as a list of routine duties.

Focus on:

- what you contributed;
- what tools you used;
- what you learnt;
- what problems you helped solve;
- and what outcomes you supported.

## Weak Example

**Finance Intern**

- Assisted with accounting work.
- Prepared reports.
- Worked with the finance team.
- Filed documents.

## Stronger Example

**Finance Intern — ABC Manufacturing Ltd**  
May 2025–August 2025

- Reconciled supplier statements against internal records and identified discrepancies for review.
- Updated weekly expense trackers in Excel and supported preparation of departmental budget reports.
- Organised more than 300 payment documents, improving retrieval during internal audit preparation.
- Assisted the finance team with invoice verification and payment-status follow-up.

The stronger version shows scope, tools, contribution, and context.

## Bullet-Point Formula

Use this structure:

**Action + task + method/tool + result or purpose**

Example:

> Analysed customer feedback from 250 survey responses using Excel and summarised recurring service issues for the branch manager.

This is stronger than:

> Helped with customer surveys.

# Referees

Include referees only when:

- the employer requests them;
- the application form requires them;
- or you have enough space and strong professional references.

Suitable referees include:

- internship supervisors;
- lecturers;
- project supervisors;
- volunteer coordinators;
- former managers.

Ask for permission before listing someone.

Provide:

- name;
- job title;
- organisation;
- phone number;
- professional email.

Avoid listing relatives or friends.

Where space is limited, you may write:

> Referees available upon request.

However, many Kenyan employers still request referee details, so keep them prepared separately.

# Tailoring Your CV to Each Graduate Programme

A generic CV may be acceptable for initial preparation.

It should not be the final version you submit.

Each programme has different priorities.

A bank may value numerical reasoning, customer awareness, and commercial judgement.

An NGO may prioritise community engagement, reporting, adaptability, and stakeholder communication.

A technology company may focus on projects, coding ability, and problem-solving.

A government programme may emphasise eligibility, public-service values, documentation, and relevant qualifications.

## The MATCH Framework

Use this five-step process before every application:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **M — Map requirements** | Identify qualifications, competencies, and keywords in the vacancy |
| **A — Align evidence** | Match your projects, experience, and skills to those requirements |
| **T — Tailor language** | Use accurate wording that reflects the employer's priorities |
| **C — Check gaps** | Identify missing evidence or unclear sections |
| **H — Highlight relevance** | Move the strongest relevant information higher on the CV |

## Example

Suppose a graduate programme requires:

- analytical thinking;
- teamwork;
- communication;
- and digital skills.

Do not simply add those exact terms to the skills section.

Instead, show evidence:

- analytical thinking through a research or data project;
- teamwork through a group assignment or internship;
- communication through presentations or stakeholder engagement;
- digital skills through specific tools.

This creates a more persuasive and credible application.

# ATS Optimisation

Many large employers use Applicant Tracking Systems to manage applications.

An ATS may scan your CV for:

- degree requirements;
- skills;
- job titles;
- keywords;
- dates;
- and relevant experience.

You should optimise for both software and human readers.

## ATS-Friendly Practices

- Use clear headings.
- Use standard fonts.
- Avoid tables for the entire layout.
- Avoid text boxes.
- Avoid graphics and icons.
- Use keywords naturally.
- Save in the requested format.
- Keep dates consistent.
- Use simple bullet points.
- Spell out important terms where necessary.

## Avoid

- heavily designed templates;
- multiple columns that read out of order;
- unusual headings;
- skill-rating bars;
- icons without text;
- images containing important information;
- and decorative backgrounds.

A visually attractive CV is not useful if the system cannot interpret it properly.

# Writing Strong Bullet Points

Strong bullet points show contribution.

Weak bullet points merely name activities.

## Weak

> Participated in marketing activities.

## Stronger

> Supported a three-week campus marketing campaign, coordinated student outreach, and helped increase event registrations from 120 to 210.

## Weak

> Worked with customers.

## Stronger

> Assisted an average of 25 customers per day, resolved routine account queries, and escalated complex cases to the appropriate officer.

## Weak

> Responsible for data entry.

## Stronger

> Entered and verified more than 800 client records in the department database while checking for incomplete information and duplicate entries.

Use numbers where they are accurate and helpful.

Do not invent metrics.

# Common Graduate CV Mistakes

## Using a Generic CV

A CV that tries to suit every employer usually becomes relevant to none.

Tailor it.

## Writing Duties Instead of Achievements

Recruiters want to understand your contribution, not just the department where you worked.

## Including Irrelevant Personal Information

Do not include details that do not strengthen the application.

## Using Decorative Templates

Complex designs may reduce readability and ATS compatibility.

## Making Unsupported Claims

Do not write “excellent leader” without evidence.

## Listing Every Course or Skill

Focus on the most relevant information.

## Using One CV for Different Industries

A bank, NGO, technology company, and manufacturing employer may value different evidence.

## Submitting Without Proofreading

Spelling errors and inconsistent dates reduce confidence in your attention to detail.

## Exaggerating Experience

Recruiters may test your claims during interviews or reference checks.

Always remain accurate.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## How long should a graduate programme CV be?

Usually one to two pages.

Use one page when your experience is limited and the content remains complete.

Use two pages when you have substantial internships, leadership, projects, certifications, or volunteering.

## Should I include a photograph?

Not unless the employer specifically requests one.

## Should I include my national ID number?

No, unless required at a later application stage.

## Can I apply without internship experience?

Yes.

Use academic projects, volunteering, leadership, freelance work, personal projects, and extracurricular activities to demonstrate potential.

## Should I include my KCSE grade?

Include it where the programme requests secondary-school results or where it adds useful context.

## Do I need a different CV for every employer?

You need a tailored version for each serious application.

The overall structure may remain similar, but the summary, evidence order, keywords, and emphasis should reflect the programme.

## What file name should I use?

Use a professional format such as:

`Amina_Njeri_Graduate_Trainee_CV.pdf`

Avoid vague names such as:

`cvfinal2.pdf`

## Should I send Word or PDF?

Follow the employer's instructions.

PDF usually preserves formatting, but some systems request Word documents.

## Can I use a CV template?

Yes, provided it is simple, readable, professional, and ATS-friendly.

## What if my grades are not strong?

Do not misrepresent them.

Strengthen the CV through practical projects, technical skills, leadership, volunteering, and relevant certifications.

## Should I list hobbies?

Only when they provide relevant insight or demonstrate sustained achievement.

For example, competitive sport, debate, coding, writing, or community work may add value.

Generic hobbies usually do not.

---

# Key Takeaways

A strong graduate programme CV should:

- prove potential through specific evidence;
- focus on relevance;
- show achievements rather than vague duties;
- present education, projects, internships, and leadership clearly;
- remain easy to scan;
- include appropriate keywords;
- and be tailored to each employer.

You do not need years of experience to create a competitive CV.

You need to present the experience you already have with clarity, judgement, and evidence.

---

# Final Thoughts

Graduate programme recruitment is competitive because many applicants have similar academic qualifications.

Your CV must help the recruiter understand what makes your potential different.

That difference may come from a project you completed, a problem you solved during attachment, a student organisation you helped lead, a technical skill you developed independently, or a community initiative you supported.

None of those experiences will help you if they are buried beneath generic language.

Build your CV around evidence.

Show what you did.

Explain how you contributed.

Make the relevance clear.

Tailor the document carefully.

A good graduate programme CV does not try to make you look like a senior professional.

It shows that you are ready to learn, contribute, and grow.
