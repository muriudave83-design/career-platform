# Graduate Programme vs Temporary Job in Kenya (2026): Which Is Better for Your Career?

You have graduated.

You are applying for graduate programmes, entry-level positions and other early-career opportunities.

Then a temporary job becomes available.

Perhaps it lasts:

- several weeks;
- three months;
- six months;
- for the duration of a particular assignment;
- until a specific short-term organisational need ends.

At the same time, you are pursuing—or perhaps have already received—an opportunity to join a graduate programme.

What should you do?

Should you take the temporary job because it gives you:

- immediate income;
- workplace experience;
- something useful to do while searching;
- potential professional contacts?

Or should you prioritise the graduate programme because it may provide:

- structured development;
- mentorship;
- rotations;
- more deliberate career progression;
- a clearer early-career pathway?

The answer depends heavily on one question:

> **What role is the temporary job playing in your career strategy?**

A temporary job can be a useful bridge.

It can also become a distraction.

It can provide highly relevant professional experience.

Or it can provide income without significantly advancing your intended career.

A graduate programme can offer excellent structured development.

But it should not automatically be preferred simply because it carries the words **graduate programme**.

The right comparison is not:

> **temporary = bad, graduate programme = good.**

It is:

> **Which opportunity gives me the stronger combination of immediate value, professional development and future career options?**

---

# Quick Answer

A **graduate programme** is generally a structured early-career pathway designed specifically for eligible graduates.

Depending on the employer, it may include:

- training;
- mentorship;
- rotations;
- projects;
- assessments;
- increasing responsibility;
- eventual placement into a substantive role.

A **temporary job** is employment intended to meet a limited-duration need.

The exact structure varies.

A temporary role might give you:

- immediate income;
- professional exposure;
- relevant experience;
- transferable skills;
- a reference;
- a bridge while pursuing longer-term opportunities.

But its career value depends heavily on the actual work.

If the temporary job is directly relevant to your intended profession and provides substantive responsibility, it may be highly valuable.

If it provides little professional development, its main value may instead be:

- income;
- work routine;
- short-term stability.

That can still matter.

The decision should therefore consider:

**career relevance + skills + responsibility + income + duration + opportunity cost + next-step value.**

# First: What Is a Temporary Job?

A temporary job is employment intended to cover a limited period or temporary organisational need.

The exact arrangement can differ significantly between employers.

A business may need additional support because of:

- seasonal demand;
- a short-term project;
- temporary workload increases;
- an event;
- data collection;
- administrative backlog;
- another defined operational need.

Your responsibilities could therefore range from highly professional work to basic short-term support.

That range is important.

Two temporary jobs can have completely different career value.

---

# Temporary Job vs Contract Job: Are They the Same?

These terms can sometimes overlap in everyday conversation.

An employer may describe a fixed-duration role as:

- temporary;
- fixed-term;
- contract;
- project-based.

But you should not assume that every employer uses the terminology identically.

Instead of making your decision from the label, examine the actual arrangement.

Ask:

- What role am I being hired to perform?
- How long is the employment expected to last?
- What does the employment documentation say?
- What responsibilities will I have?
- What happens when the temporary need ends?

The substance matters more than terminology.

---

# Temporary Job Does Not Automatically Mean Casual or Unskilled Work

A temporary opportunity can involve meaningful professional responsibility.

For example, a graduate might temporarily work as a:

- Research Assistant;
- Data Assistant;
- Project Assistant;
- Finance Assistant;
- Communications Assistant;
- Junior Developer;
- Field Researcher;
- Administrative Assistant.

Some of these roles may provide direct experience relevant to a graduate's intended profession.

Others may primarily provide income and general workplace exposure.

Both can have value.

But the value is different.

---

# What Is a Graduate Programme?

A graduate programme is usually designed around professional development.

Rather than hiring you only to solve an immediate short-term staffing need, the employer may intentionally develop you over a defined programme period.

A typical pathway might resemble:

**Recruitment → Onboarding → Training → Rotations → Projects → Assessment → Completion → Placement or Next Stage**

Not every programme follows this structure.

Some are rotational.

Others are specialist.

Some provide substantial formal training.

Others rely heavily on practical assignments.

Always investigate the specific programme.

---

# The Fundamental Difference

The distinction can be simplified like this:

### Graduate Programme

The organisation is intentionally investing in your **early-career development** through a structured pathway.

### Temporary Job

The organisation primarily needs someone to perform **work for a limited period or temporary need**.

That does not make the temporary role inferior.

It simply means you may need to take more responsibility for turning the opportunity into career capital.

# Why Temporary Jobs Can Be Useful After Graduation

Graduation does not always lead immediately into your ideal first professional role.

Recruitment takes time.

Graduate programme applications may involve:

- online assessments;
- interviews;
- assessment centres;
- several recruitment stages;
- long waiting periods.

During that period, temporary work can provide useful value.

---

# Benefit 1: Immediate Income

The most obvious benefit is financial.

A graduate job search can involve real expenses.

These may include:

- internet access;
- transport;
- interview clothing;
- document preparation;
- relocation planning;
- ordinary living costs.

Temporary work may help you support yourself while continuing your search.

That is a legitimate benefit.

---

# Benefit 2: Work Routine

Moving from university into an extended job search can create a major change in routine.

Temporary employment may help you maintain:

- punctuality;
- professional communication;
- workplace discipline;
- responsibility;
- time management.

These habits can be useful even when the job itself is not directly related to your intended profession.

---

# Benefit 3: Professional Evidence

A relevant temporary job can give you new examples for:

- CVs;
- interviews;
- applications.

Instead of discussing only university assignments, you may be able to discuss:

- real deadlines;
- customers;
- colleagues;
- organisational systems;
- projects;
- workplace problems.

That can strengthen your professional profile.

# Benefit 5: Industry Exposure

A temporary role inside your target industry can provide valuable context even when the exact job is not your intended long-term position.

You may learn:

- industry terminology;
- organisational processes;
- customer needs;
- professional standards;
- common technologies.

That exposure can improve future applications and interviews.

# Benefit 6: Professional Relationships

Temporary employees can still build meaningful professional relationships.

If you:

That can become valuable professional capital.

# When Temporary Work Becomes Strategically Strong

Temporary work becomes particularly attractive when several conditions align.

For example:

- the role is relevant to your intended career;
- you will perform substantive work;
- the duration fits your plans;
- the income is useful;
- you can continue pursuing appropriate longer-term opportunities;
- the experience strengthens your next application.

In that situation, the temporary role is doing more than filling time.

It is building momentum.

# Scenario 3: Temporary Job vs Confirmed Graduate Programme Offer

You need to consider **opportunity cost**.

If taking the temporary role means losing a strong graduate programme aligned with your career, the temporary income may not justify the longer-term cost.

# Opportunity Cost Matters

Opportunity cost means considering what you give up when choosing one option.

A temporary job may provide:

- KSh income;
- immediate work;
- short-term experience.

But if accepting it causes you to lose:

- a strong graduate programme;
- a highly relevant permanent role;
- a major professional opportunity;

the real cost is much larger than it first appears.

---

# JoinNexiva Framework: The OPPORTUNITY COST Question

# Do Not Reject Temporary Work Because Something Better Might Happen

The opposite mistake is also common.

A graduate may reject useful temporary work because:

> **"I applied for several graduate programmes."**

But an application is not an offer.

Until you have a confirmed alternative, evaluate the temporary opportunity on its own merits.

You can often continue pursuing suitable longer-term opportunities while working, subject to your actual commitments.

---

# JoinNexiva Principle: Compare Offers With Offers

Do not compare:

**confirmed temporary job**

against:

**graduate programme application.**

Those are not equivalent.

One is an opportunity you currently have.

The other is a possibility.

A better comparison is:

> **Is this temporary job worthwhile while I continue pursuing my longer-term career strategy?**

If a graduate programme offer later arrives, you can make a new decision using the facts available then.

- meaningful responsibility;

- credible progression;

- short-term income;
- limited professional relevance;

# When the Temporary Job May Deserve Serious Consideration

The temporary job may be particularly attractive when it provides:

- direct experience in your intended profession;
- substantial responsibility;
- valuable technical skills;
- strong supervision;

while the graduate programme:

- is poorly aligned;
- has vague responsibilities;
- provides weak development;
- has unclear outcomes.

# The First Decision Rule

Do not ask only:

> **"Which opportunity lasts longer?"**

> **"Which opportunity moves my career forward more effectively during the time I spend there?"**

Then compare:

**relevance → learning → responsibility → income → opportunity cost → next step.**

That is the foundation for deciding whether temporary work is a useful bridge or a career detour.
# Training and Development: Graduate Programme vs Temporary Job

The biggest developmental difference between these opportunities is usually **intent**.

A graduate programme is normally designed to develop eligible graduates.

A temporary job is normally created because an organisation needs work completed for a limited period.

That distinction matters.

But it does not automatically determine which opportunity will teach you more.

A temporary employee doing substantive professional work every day may develop faster than a graduate trainee receiving weak assignments.

A well-designed graduate programme may provide far more development than a temporary role built around repetitive tasks.

Compare what actually happens after you join.

- technical training;
- professional-skills development;
- mentorship;
- rotations;
- project assignments;

- assessments;

- increasing familiarity with the role.

For example, three months of meaningful data-analysis work may strengthen your practical skills more than three months of generic professional workshops.

The work itself matters.

# Temporary Job vs Graduate Programme: Breadth and Depth

Graduate programmes—especially rotational ones—may provide greater breadth.

Temporary jobs often place you into one immediate function.

This can create greater depth if the work is substantive.

---

# Breadth Can Help You Explore

Suppose you studied business administration but are unsure whether you prefer:

- Finance;
- Marketing;
- Operations;
- Sales;
- Supply Chain.

A rotational programme may allow you to experience several areas.

That information can help you make a better specialisation decision.

---

# Depth Can Help You Specialise

Suppose you already know you want to become a procurement professional.

A temporary procurement role involving:

- purchase orders;
- supplier communication;
- sourcing support;
- procurement systems;
- documentation;

may give you useful specialist experience immediately.

In that situation, temporary status does not eliminate the value of depth.

---

# The Specialisation Question

Ask:

> **Do I currently need exploration or deeper professional practice?**

If you need exploration, a rotational programme may have an advantage.

If you already know your direction, a relevant temporary role may be more valuable than its short duration initially suggests.

---

# Responsibility in Temporary Work

Temporary employees may sometimes receive clearly bounded responsibilities because the employer needs immediate support.

For example:

> "We need someone to maintain this dataset for three months."

or:

> "We need additional project support until implementation is complete."

This clarity can help you understand exactly what you are there to accomplish.

---

# The Risk of Being Used Only for Overflow Work

Temporary roles can also become heavily focused on tasks that permanent staff do not have time to complete.

That is not automatically bad.

Junior professionals often begin with routine work.

The important question is whether you are also developing.

Ask:

- Am I learning the process behind the task?
- Am I becoming more independent?
- Am I developing transferable skills?
- Am I gaining useful professional evidence?

Routine work can still teach you.

But prolonged repetition without learning has declining career value.

---

# The Risk of Passive Graduate Programme Rotations

- attend meetings;
- complete minor support tasks.

Breadth without responsibility can become superficial.

Therefore compare:

> **What will I actually do?**

not:

> **How many departments will I see?**

# Salary and Immediate Financial Needs

Temporary work may become especially attractive when you need immediate income.

That is not poor career planning.

Financial reality is part of career planning.

But distinguish between:

> **I am taking this temporary job because I need income right now.**

and:

> **This temporary job is the best long-term professional opportunity available to me.**

Those are different claims.

You can accept the first without pretending the second is true.

# The Temporary-Job Drift Problem

This happens when a graduate accepts temporary work for a short period and then stops actively managing the longer-term career transition.

One temporary role becomes another.

Then another.

Months or years pass.

The problem is not temporary employment itself.

The problem is **lack of progression**.

---

# JoinNexiva Framework: The DRIFT Check

Every few months, ask:

## D — Direction

Am I moving toward the career I want?

## R — Responsibility

Is my professional responsibility increasing?

## I — Improvement

Are my skills improving?

## F — Future Options

Are stronger opportunities becoming available to me?

## T — Time

Is the amount of time I am investing justified by the value I am receiving?

If several answers are consistently weak, reassess your strategy.

---

without asking whether you are developing substantive professional capability.

Your goal is not merely to complete the programme.

It is to become more capable.

# Time Is Career Capital

But over meaningful periods, you should see movement in:

- skill;

- evidence;
- employability.

# Common Mistakes When Choosing Between a Graduate Programme and a Temporary Job

Temporary employment can be useful.

Graduate programmes can be useful.

The problem begins when you evaluate either opportunity using assumptions instead of evidence.

Here are the mistakes to avoid.

---

# Mistake 1: Assuming Temporary Work Has No Career Value

Temporary does not mean worthless.

A short role may provide:

- professional experience;
- technical skills;
- workplace exposure;
- income;
- references;
- stronger interview examples.

Evaluate what you will actually do.

---

# Mistake 2: Assuming Every Temporary Job Is a Career Opportunity

The opposite mistake is equally important.

Some temporary jobs may primarily provide:

- income;
- routine;
- general workplace experience.

That can still make them worthwhile.

But do not exaggerate their career-specific value.

Be clear about what the opportunity is doing for you.

---

# Mistake 3: Rejecting Real Work Because You Are Waiting for a Graduate Programme

An application is not an offer.

You may have:

- passed an online assessment;
- completed an interview;
- reached a final recruitment stage.

That is encouraging.

But until you receive a confirmed offer, uncertainty remains.

Do not automatically reject a useful opportunity because another application might succeed.

---

# Mistake 4: Accepting Temporary Work That Forces You to Lose a Much Stronger Confirmed Opportunity

Once you have two confirmed opportunities, the calculation changes.

If accepting temporary work prevents you from joining a highly aligned graduate programme, account for that opportunity cost.

Compare:

**what you gain**

against:

**what you give up.**

---

# Mistake 5: Looking Only at the Monthly Salary

A temporary job may pay more in the short term.

That matters.

But compare:

- total expected income;
- duration;
- career relevance;
- development;
- next-step opportunities.

The highest monthly figure is not necessarily the highest total career value.

---

# Mistake 6: Pretending Salary Does Not Matter

Do not make the opposite mistake.

You may need to finance:

- housing;
- food;
- transport;
- family responsibilities;
- your continuing job search.

Immediate income can be strategically important.

Career advice that ignores financial reality is incomplete.

---

# Mistake 7: Ignoring the End Date

Temporary work has a limited horizon by definition.

Understand it.

Do not wait until the final week to ask:

> **"What am I going to do next?"**

Plan ahead.

---

# Mistake 8: Becoming Obsessed With the End Date

Do not spend the entire job worrying about when it will end.

Your immediate objective is to:

- learn;
- contribute;
- build evidence;
- strengthen your professional profile.

Use the time well.

---

# Mistake 9: Assuming Temporary Means It Will Become Permanent

It may or may not.

Do not construct your career plan around an outcome that has not been confirmed.

If another opportunity later emerges, evaluate it then.

---

# Mistake 10: Assuming Graduate Programme Means Permanent Employment

Apply the same standard to graduate programmes.

Understand:

- programme duration;
- completion requirements;
- placement process;
- actual employment terms.

Do not replace missing information with optimistic assumptions.

---

# Mistake 11: Staying Because the Temporary Role Is Comfortable

Comfort can become inertia.

A temporary job may provide:

- familiar colleagues;
- predictable work;
- reliable income.

Those are real benefits.

But periodically ask whether you are still progressing.

Use the **DRIFT Check**:

**Direction → Responsibility → Improvement → Future Options → Time.**

---

# Mistake 12: Leaving Too Quickly Because Something Sounds More Prestigious

A famous graduate programme may sound impressive.

But compare it with the temporary work you are actually doing.

If your current role provides:

- strong specialist experience;
- substantive responsibility;
- excellent mentorship;
- meaningful achievements;

do not dismiss that value simply because another opportunity has a stronger brand.

---

# Mistake 13: Treating All Experience as Equal

Six months of experience can mean very different things.

One graduate may spend six months:

- performing repetitive tasks;
- learning little;
- receiving minimal responsibility.

Another may spend six months:

- working on major projects;
- developing technical skills;
- receiving feedback;
- increasing responsibility.

Duration alone does not measure development.

---

# Mistake 14: Failing to Learn Because the Job Is Temporary

Do not mentally tell yourself:

> **"I am only here for three months."**

Three months can still contain hundreds of working hours.

Use them.

Learn:

- systems;
- processes;
- professional standards;
- technical skills;
- communication practices.

---

# Mistake 15: Performing Poorly Because You Expect to Leave

Your employment duration does not reduce your responsibility to perform professionally.

A temporary employee can still build a strong reputation.

And a poor reputation can also follow you.

Do the work properly.

---

# Mistake 16: Stopping Your Career Development Outside Work

If the temporary role is not aligned with your intended career, continue developing relevant capability where realistic.

That may include:

- portfolio work;
- professional courses;
- technical practice;
- applications;
- interview preparation.

Do not expect an unrelated temporary role to build skills it was never designed to provide.

---

# Mistake 17: Trying to Do Everything at Once

There is also a limit.

Working full time while simultaneously:

- completing multiple courses;
- submitting dozens of applications;
- building several projects;
- attending every networking event;

may become unsustainable.

Prioritise.

A focused career strategy is usually stronger than constant activity.

---

# Mistake 18: Ignoring Eligibility Windows

If you intend to apply for graduate programmes later, check actual eligibility criteria when programmes open.

Do not assume you will remain eligible indefinitely.

Requirements may relate to:

- graduation date;
- qualifications;
- experience;
- other programme-specific criteria.

---

# Mistake 19: Hiding Temporary Experience From Your CV Because It Was Short

If the experience is relevant and strengthens your application, short duration alone does not necessarily make it useless.

Represent the role accurately.

Focus on:

- responsibilities;
- achievements;
- skills.

---

# Mistake 20: Filling Your CV With Every Very Short Job Regardless of Relevance

The opposite problem also exists.

Your CV is not necessarily a complete diary of every activity you have ever performed.

Present your experience appropriately for the application while remaining truthful.

Prioritise relevant professional evidence.

---

# Mistake 21: Describing Temporary Work Apologetically

Do not write or speak as though:

> **"It was only temporary."**

Instead explain what happened.

For example:

> "I joined the operations team for a four-month peak-period assignment and was responsible for maintaining daily order records and resolving reporting discrepancies."

The duration is context.

The work is the substance.

---

# Mistake 22: Failing to Ask for Feedback

Even if you will be there briefly, feedback can help you identify:

- strengths;
- weaknesses;
- technical gaps;
- professional habits to improve.

A short opportunity can still produce long-term learning.

---

# Mistake 23: Failing to Record Achievements

At the end of several months, you may forget:

- projects;
- results;
- responsibilities;
- improvements.

Maintain appropriate records of your own professional achievements without retaining confidential employer information.

This will make future CV writing and interview preparation easier.

---

# Mistake 24: Leaving Unprofessionally When a Better Opportunity Arrives

Excitement about a graduate programme does not remove your existing obligations.

Review the relevant terms.

Communicate professionally.

Complete an appropriate handover where required.

How you leave is part of your professional reputation.

**Income Bridge → Career-Aligned Opportunity**

2. manage your finances;
3. continue targeted applications where appropriate;
4. maintain relevant professional development;
5. prepare for the next transition.

The temporary job becomes part of the strategy rather than evidence that the strategy failed.

- Which gives me stronger technical work?
- Which provides greater responsibility?
- Which gives better supervision?
- Which develops skills employers value?
- Which has better financial terms?
- Which provides stronger next-step options?

The word **temporary** should not decide the answer.

# How to Decide When Neither Opportunity Is Ideal

Sometimes graduates are choosing between imperfect options.

Perhaps:

### Temporary Job

Provides income but little career relevance.

### Graduate Programme

Provides some training but does not align well with your intended profession.

You may still need to choose one.

Ask which option:

- solves the most urgent current problem;
- creates the fewest long-term constraints;
- provides the most transferable value;
- leaves you best positioned to pursue stronger opportunities.

You do not need to pretend an imperfect opportunity is perfect.

Use it strategically.

- no stronger offer is confirmed;
- the role provides useful experience;
- the schedule remains manageable;
- the work strengthens your professional profile.

Do not confuse waiting with career progress simply because you are waiting for a prestigious opportunity.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is a graduate programme better than a temporary job in Kenya?

Not automatically.

A strong graduate programme may provide more structured:

- training;
- mentorship;
- rotations;
- progression.

A relevant temporary job may provide:

- immediate professional experience;
- income;
- specialist skills;
- substantive responsibility.

Compare the actual opportunities.

---

## Is temporary work bad for a fresh graduate?

No.

Temporary work can provide useful:

- income;
- workplace experience;
- skills;
- professional evidence.

Its value depends on the work and how it fits your wider career strategy.

---

## Should I take a temporary job while applying for graduate programmes?

It can be reasonable if the job provides useful value and you can continue managing your graduate programme applications appropriately.

A pending application is not the same as a confirmed offer.

---

## Should I reject a temporary job if I have reached the final stage of a graduate programme application?

Not automatically.

Final-stage recruitment still does not necessarily mean you have an offer.

Evaluate the temporary opportunity while continuing the recruitment process.

If an offer later arrives, reassess using the confirmed information.

---

## Should I leave a temporary job if I receive a graduate programme offer?

Compare the opportunities first.

If the graduate programme provides substantially stronger career value and you decide to move, review your existing obligations and handle the transition professionally.

---

## Can I take temporary work before a graduate programme starts?

Potentially, yes, if the timing and employment commitments genuinely allow it.

Review both opportunities carefully before committing.

---

## Does temporary experience look bad on a CV?

Temporary status alone does not make professional experience bad.

Focus on what you:

- did;
- learned;
- achieved.

Represent the arrangement accurately.

---

## How do I explain a temporary job in an interview?

Explain:

- why the role existed where relevant;
- what you were responsible for;
- what you achieved;
- what you learned.

Keep the explanation professional and factual.

---

## What if the temporary job is unrelated to my degree?

It may still provide:

- income;
- transferable skills;
- workplace experience.

But recognise that you may still need career-specific experience for your intended profession.

---

## Can temporary work lead to permanent employment?

Another opportunity may sometimes arise, depending on the employer and circumstances.

Do not assume this will happen unless it is actually confirmed.

Evaluate the temporary job based on its current value.

---

## What if my temporary job keeps getting extended?

Reassess each extension.

Ask whether you are gaining:

- new skills;
- greater responsibility;
- better terms;
- stronger future options.

Continuation is not automatically progression.

---

## Should I stay in temporary work because the employer might eventually hire me permanently?

Do not base your entire career strategy on an uncertain future possibility.

Evaluate:

- current development;
- actual terms;
- alternative opportunities;
- long-term alignment.

---

## Is a graduate programme more secure than temporary work?

A temporary job has a deliberately limited horizon.

A graduate programme may provide a longer structured pathway, but you should still understand what happens after programme completion.

Do not assume future employment arrangements without checking.

---

## What if the temporary job pays more than the graduate programme?

Compare both present and future value.

Higher temporary pay may be highly important.

But also consider:

- duration;
- career relevance;
- training;
- responsibility;
- progression;
- next-step opportunities.

---

## What if I urgently need money?

Financial sustainability is a legitimate career consideration.

Temporary work can function as an income bridge while you continue building toward your intended career.

Perform the role professionally while keeping your longer-term direction clear.

---

## How long should I stay in temporary work?

There is no universal period.

Evaluate whether you are still gaining sufficient:

- income;
- experience;
- skills;
- responsibility;
- career options.

Use the **DRIFT Check** periodically.

---

## Should I include a one-month temporary job on my CV?

It depends on whether the experience is relevant and useful to the application.

A CV should prioritise strong, relevant evidence while remaining truthful.

---

## Is it better to be unemployed and wait for a graduate programme?

Not automatically.

If you have no confirmed programme offer and a worthwhile temporary opportunity is available, temporary work may provide useful income and experience.

Your circumstances and the quality of the opportunity matter.

---

# Key Takeaways

A graduate programme and a temporary job serve different purposes.

A graduate programme is usually designed around:

- structured graduate development;
- training;
- mentorship;
- rotations or specialist assignments;
- increasing responsibility;
- progression toward a next professional stage.

A temporary job is usually created to meet a limited-duration organisational need.

But temporary work can still provide substantial:

- professional experience;
- income;
- transferable skills;
- specialist capability;
- workplace exposure;
- career momentum.

The most important question is not:

> **"Is the job temporary?"**

It is:

> **"What does this temporary period help me build?"**

Use the **BRIDGE Test** to evaluate whether temporary work builds something useful, remains relevant, provides necessary income, fits the required duration, supports growth and leads toward a stronger exit.

Use the **LEARNING Test** to compare actual developmental quality.

Use the **TWO-WIN Strategy** to look for temporary opportunities that create both immediate and future value.

Use the **DRIFT Check** to ensure temporary work does not become repeated continuation without progression.

Use the **SEQUENCE Test** when temporary work and a graduate programme may fit together rather than compete.

And use the **TEMPORARY Test** when you have to choose between actual offers.

Most importantly:

> **A temporary job can be a bridge without becoming your destination.**

If it gives you:

**income + experience + skills + professional evidence + momentum**

then it may be a valuable part of your career journey.

And if a graduate programme offers substantially stronger:

**development + alignment + responsibility + progression**

then recognise the opportunity cost of giving it up for short-term convenience.

Choose the path that solves today's real needs while strengthening tomorrow's professional options.
